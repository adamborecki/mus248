## 🎚️ Core skill area

**Audio Capture** and **Video Capture** — every recording starts with a cable, and the wrong one is the most common reason a recital does not get recorded.

## 🌍 Why this matters

Somebody hands you a microphone, a camera, and a bin of cables, and the concert starts in ten minutes. You do not get to read a manual. You have to look at the two ends of a cable and know what it is, what it connects, and whether the thing you need is the thing in your hand.

That is a skill you build by *looking at a lot of cables*, not by reading a list of them. This activity is a safari: a short list of cables you must be able to find and name, and then an open stretch where you go hunting for as many different kinds as you can.

## 🎯 What you’ll practice

By the end of this activity, you should be able to:

- Name a cable by its **two ends** — connector type and gender, one end at a time.
- Tell **male** from **female** connectors on sight, and say which end of an XLR cable belongs on a microphone.
- Tell **TS** from **TRS**, and the three HDMI sizes from one another.
- Say what a cable is *for*, not just what it is called.
- Find where cables live in the room, and put them back there.

## 🗝️ Key terms

**male** · **female** · **TS** · **TRS** · **adapter** · XLR · RCA · BNC · SDI · HDMI (full-size · mini · micro) · USB A / B / C · IEC · Ethernet · coupler

## 📍 Logistics

**Time:** 20–30 minutes. Part 1 should take about half of that; spend the rest on the safari.

**Groups:** Work alone or in a pair. A pair finds cables faster, but each person should be able to name what the pair found.

**Where:** BH 208 and BH 209. Look wherever cables are kept in the room; if you are not sure where that is, ask.

**What counts as a find:** you hold the cable (or look closely at the device), say both ends out loud, and write it on your worksheet. Photos are not required.

## 🧰 Equipment and materials

- [ ] The printed worksheet and something to write with
- [ ] Your phone, for the activity page (optional: a flashlight helps in a dim cabinet)
- [ ] Nothing else. Everything you need to find is already in the room.

## ⚠️ Safety and handling

- **Loose cables only.** Take cables from where they are stored. Do **not** unplug anything that is connected to a system that is on, in use, or installed — if you want to look at a plugged-in cable, look; do not pull.
- **Another group’s station is not a parts bin.** If a cable is plugged into someone else’s setup, it is not yours to take.
- **Power supplies belong to a device.** If a power adapter is connected to a device, leave it. Do not swap adapters because a connector happens to fit.
- **Handle by the connector, not the cord.** Do not yank a cable out of a bin by the middle; you will tangle the whole bin.
- **Leave the room cleaner than you found it.** Keep track of where every cable came from. When you are done, coil each one neatly and return it to the same place. If a cable is clearly in the wrong place, put it where it belongs.
- **If you do not know, do not guess.** A cable you cannot identify is a *mystery sighting* — see Part 2. Do not make up a name.

## 🐘 Part 1 — The list

For each item below, find it, hold it, and **say both ends out loud** before you tick it off. A cable can be a different length, color, or brand than you expected; what matters is the two ends.

1. 🚩 **HDMI, full-size on both ends.** The standard one that plugs into a TV or projector.
2. 🚩 **HDMI, full-size to mini HDMI.** One end is the normal size; the other is noticeably smaller.
3. 🚩 **HDMI, full-size to micro HDMI.** The smallest of the three.
4. 🚩 **An XLR cable.** Any length. Check which end has pins and which end has holes.
5. 🚩 **A device with an XLR female jack, and a device with an XLR male jack.** Look at them where they are; do not unplug anything. Think about what plugs into each: a microphone, a mixer input, an interface, a speaker.
6. 🚩 **XLR to TRS.** One end is an XLR; the other is a 1/4-inch plug with two bands on it.
7. 🚩 **An instrument cable.** A 1/4-inch TS plug on both ends — the kind a guitar uses.
8. 🚩 **3.5 mm to dual RCA.** One small stereo plug splitting into two RCA plugs.
9. 🚩 **An SDI cable.** The ends are BNC connectors — round, with a twist-lock collar.
10. 🚩 **An Ethernet cable.** Eight-pin plugs, a latch clip on each end.
11. 🚩 **A USB A to USB B cable.** One flat rectangular end, one squarer end.
12. 🚩 **A 3-prong IEC power cable.** The kind that plugs into the back of a mixer, an amplifier, or a computer power supply.

**Q1 (fill in the blank).** Where each kind of cable lives, so you can put it back: HDMI ________________ · XLR ________________ · USB ________________ · power ________________

✅ **Answer.** The student’s own record of where each kind of cable was stored (cabinet, drawer, bin, hook, or shelf in BH 208 / BH 209).

**Q2 (multiple choice).** Put the three HDMI sizes in order, largest connector to smallest:

- a) micro, mini, full-size
- b) mini, full-size, micro
- ✅ c) full-size, mini, micro
- d) full-size, micro, mini

**Q3 (multiple choice).** A microphone has an XLR *male* connector (pins). Which end of an XLR cable plugs onto the microphone?

- a) The male end, because it matches the mic
- ✅ b) The female end
- c) Either end — XLR has no gender
- d) Neither; microphones use TRS

**Q4 (fill in the blank).** TRS stands for T______ · R______ · S______.

✅ **Answer.** Tip · Ring · Sleeve.

**Q5 (multiple choice).** A 3.5 mm to dual RCA cable ends in two RCA plugs. Why two?

- ✅ a) One carries the left channel and one the right
- b) One is signal and one is power
- c) One is a spare in case the first breaks
- d) One is for video and one is for audio

> 🚩 **Checkpoint.** All twelve items are ticked, and you could say both ends of each one without looking at it.

## 🦁 Part 2 — The safari

Now the list is over and the room is yours. **Find at least eight more kinds of cable**, adapter, or connector that are *not* on the list above.

**What counts as a different kind:** two cables are the same kind if both ends match, connector for connector. A 6-foot XLR and a 25-foot XLR are the same kind. A black USB cable and a white one are the same kind. A cable with the same connectors but one end swapped is a *different* kind. An **adapter** (a gender changer, a coupler, a plug that turns one connector into another) counts as its own find — and often a good one.

**For every sighting, write down:**

1. **Ends** — the connector and gender at each end. “XLR female → 1/4-inch TRS,” not “a mic cable.”
2. **Habitat** — what it normally connects to what: “a microphone to a mixer.”

If you cannot name a connector, do **not** guess. Write **mystery** and describe it (shape, number of pins, size). A mystery is a legitimate sighting; bring it to your instructor and ask.

## ✅ Definition of done

- 🚩 I found and named both ends of all twelve cables in Part 1.
- 🚩 I logged at least eight different kinds of cable in Part 2, each with both ends and a habitat.
- 🚩 I never unplugged anything from a system that was on, in use, or installed.
- 🚩 I coiled every cable I took and returned it to the place I wrote down in Q1.
- 🚩 I added my sightings and a short note to my journal for this week’s report.

## ⭐ Explore if time allows

Only after the core path is done and everything is put away.

1. 🚩 **Rare Specimen.** Choose the strangest or most unusual cable you found. Name both ends, say what it is for, and say why you think the room owns it.
2. 🚩 **Another habitat — BH 102 (Salmon storage).** Ask before you go. Compare what is stored there to what you found in BH 208 / BH 209. What is different, and why do you think it is?
3. 🚩 **Beyond eight.** Keep going. Count your total unique kinds.

**Q14 (short answer).** Rare Specimen: name the cable, name both ends, say what it is for, and say why you think the room owns it.

✅ **Answer.** The student’s own cable. Full credit for both ends named correctly, a plausible use, and a reason that connects to the kind of work done in the room. A confident wrong name is a miss; “I could not identify it” plus a careful description is full credit.

**Q15 (short answer).** BH 102 compared to BH 208 / BH 209: what kinds of cable did you find there that were not here, and what do you think that tells you about how the space is used?

✅ **Answer.** The student’s own comparison. Full credit for at least one concrete difference and a reasoned guess about why.

## 🧹 Finish, reset, put away

1. 🚩 Coil every cable you took.
2. 🚩 Return each cable to the place you wrote down in Q1.
3. 🚩 If you found a cable clearly in the wrong place, put it where it belongs.
4. 🚩 Check that nothing you unplugged to look at — if you unplugged anything at all — is back the way you found it.

> 🚩 **Final checkpoint.** Every cable you took is back where it came from, and the room is tidier than when you arrived.
