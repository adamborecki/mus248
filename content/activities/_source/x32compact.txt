OH B01 — X32 Compact PA Setup & Dante Practice System
Goal
Starting with the X32 Compact in an unknown configuration, build a complete working PA system with:
* 3 wireless microphones
* 2 M-Audio Studiophile AV40 speakers as Main L/R
* 2 Asceno Acoustics passive speakers as independent monitors
* Bus 1 controlling Monitor 1
* Bus 2 controlling Monitor 2
* Main L/R routed to X32 Outputs 7 and 8
* 8 separate stems from Logic routed through Dante to X32 Channels 9–16
At the end, you should be able to:
* Pair the wireless microphones with the Phenyx Pro PTU-6000 receiver
* Get all 3 microphones into the X32
* Send all 3 microphones to Main L/R
* Build two different monitor mixes using Bus 1 and Bus 2
* Independently control Main L/R, Bus 1, and Bus 2
* Use Sends on Fader
* Route 8 separate Logic stems through Dante
* Receive those stems on X32 Channels 9–16
* Explain the basic signal flow through the entire system
________________


PART 1 — BASIC PA SETUP
Step 1 — Read This Before Factory Resetting
Normally, DO NOT factory reset an X32.
A factory reset can erase routing, scenes, presets, and other settings that someone may have spent a long time creating.
For this OH B01 exercise only, we intentionally want to start from a clean/default console.
Before continuing:
* Confirm that you are working on the OH B01 X32 Compact
* Confirm that this exercise specifically tells you to factory reset it
* Make sure the M-Audio speakers and QSC power amplifier are OFF
________________


Step 2 — Factory Reset the X32 Compact
On the X32 Compact:
1. Press SETUP.
2. Go to the Global tab.
3. Find the Reset section toward the right side of the screen.
4. Scroll to Factory Reset.
5. Select it and follow the confirmation prompt.
The console should now be back in its default configuration.
________________


Step 3 — Gather the Equipment
Mixer and Wireless Equipment
* 1 × Behringer X32 Compact
* 1 × Phenyx Pro PTU-6000 wireless receiver
* 3 × Phenyx wireless handheld microphones
* 6 × AA batteries
   * 2 per microphone
Speakers and Amplifier
* 2 × M-Audio Studiophile AV40 speakers
   * Main Left and Main Right
* 2 × Asceno Acoustics passive speakers
   * Monitor 1 and Monitor 2
* 1 × QSC CX702 power amplifier
Audio Cables
* 5 × XLR cables
   * 3 for PTU-6000 → X32
   * 2 for X32 → QSC amplifier
* 2 × XLR-F to ¼-inch cables
   * X32 Output 7 → Main Left
   * X32 Output 8 → Main Right
* 3 × speaker cables
   * 1 between the M-Audio speakers
   * 1 QSC → Monitor 1
   * 1 QSC → Monitor 2
Important: Speaker cables are not the same as ordinary instrument or line-level ¼-inch cables.
________________


Step 4 — Keep Speakers and Amplifier OFF
Before making audio connections:
* M-Audio speakers OFF
* QSC CX702 OFF
Power-On Order
1. Mixer and source equipment first
2. Speakers and power amplifiers last
Power-Off Order
1. Speakers and power amplifiers first
2. Mixer and source equipment last
This reduces the chance of loud pops reaching the speakers.
________________


PART 2 — WIRELESS MICROPHONES
Step 5 — Connect the Wireless Receiver
Use three XLR cables:
* Receiver Channel 1 → X32 Input 1
* Receiver Channel 2 → X32 Input 2
* Receiver Channel 3 → X32 Input 3
Signal Flow
Mic 1 → Receiver Ch. 1 → X32 Input 1
Mic 2 → Receiver Ch. 2 → X32 Input 2
Mic 3 → Receiver Ch. 3 → X32 Input 3
________________


Step 6 — Install Batteries
Each microphone requires 2 AA batteries.
Install:
* Mic 1 — 2 AA
* Mic 2 — 2 AA
* Mic 3 — 2 AA
Turn on the PTU-6000 receiver.
Pair and test one microphone at a time.
________________


Step 7 — Pair Mic 1
Select Receiver Channel 1
1. Select Channel 1 on the PTU-6000.
2. Confirm you are editing the correct receiver channel.
Scan for a Frequency
1. Use the appropriate SET control to begin the scan.
2. Allow the receiver to search for an available frequency.
3. Wait for the scan to finish.
IR Sync
1. Turn on Mic 1.
2. Locate the IR window on the microphone.
3. Locate the IR window on the receiver.
4. Activate IR synchronization.
5. Hold the microphone’s IR window close to and directly facing the receiver’s IR window.
6. Wait for synchronization to complete.
Speak into Mic 1 and confirm Receiver Channel 1 shows activity.
________________


Step 8 — Pair Mics 2 and 3
Repeat the same procedure:
* Mic 2 → Receiver Channel 2
* Mic 3 → Receiver Channel 3
Do one microphone at a time.
When finished:
* Mic 1 communicates with Receiver Ch. 1
* Mic 2 communicates with Receiver Ch. 2
* Mic 3 communicates with Receiver Ch. 3
________________


Step 9 — Verify the Wireless Receiver First
Speak into each microphone and watch the PTU-6000.
Confirm:
* Mic 1 → activity on Receiver Ch. 1
* Mic 2 → activity on Receiver Ch. 2
* Mic 3 → activity on Receiver Ch. 3
If the receiver doesn’t show activity, troubleshoot:
* Batteries
* Frequency
* Pairing
* Microphone
If the receiver does show activity but the X32 doesn’t, troubleshoot:
* XLR cable
* X32 input
* Input routing
* Channel selection
* Gain
Important Troubleshooting Rule
Follow the signal path and find the exact point where the signal disappears.
________________


PART 3 — SPEAKER CONNECTIONS
Step 10 — Connect the Main Speakers
The M-Audio Studiophile AV40 speakers are the Main L/R speakers.
Connect:
* X32 Output 7 → Main Left
* X32 Output 8 → Main Right
Use the XLR-F to ¼-inch cables.
Connect the M-Audio speakers to one another using the appropriate speaker cable.
Keep their volume low and leave them OFF for now.
________________


Step 11 — Connect the Monitors
The Asceno speakers are passive and require the QSC CX702 amplifier.
X32 → QSC
* X32 Output 1 → QSC input for Monitor 1
* X32 Output 2 → QSC input for Monitor 2
QSC → Monitors
* QSC Channel for Monitor 1 → Asceno Monitor 1
* QSC Channel for Monitor 2 → Asceno Monitor 2
Signal Flow
Bus 1 → Output 1 → QSC → Monitor 1
Bus 2 → Output 2 → QSC → Monitor 2
________________


PART 4 — X32 INPUTS AND MAIN MIX
Step 12 — Confirm Local Input Routing
For the wireless microphones:
* X32 Channel 1 → Local Input 1
* X32 Channel 2 → Local Input 2
* X32 Channel 3 → Local Input 3
The Dante setup later will use Channels 9–16, so Channels 1–3 can remain assigned to the local microphone inputs.
________________


Step 13 — Set Microphone Gain
Start with Channel 1.
1. Select Channel 1.
2. Speak into Mic 1.
3. Watch the channel meter.
4. Adjust input gain until you have a healthy signal.
5. Avoid clipping.
Repeat for Channels 2 and 3.
Think through the signal flow:
Microphone → Wireless Receiver → X32 Input → Channel → Mix → Output → Speaker
________________


Step 14 — Send Microphones to Main L/R
For Channels 1–3:
1. Make sure each channel is assigned to Main L/R.
2. Make sure none are muted.
3. Bring the channel faders to reasonable levels.
4. Keep the Main L/R master low while testing.
The final main path is:
Channels 1–3 → Main L/R → Outputs 7 & 8 → M-Audio speakers
________________


PART 5 — OUTPUT ROUTING
Step 15 — Route the X32 Outputs
Configure the console so:
* Bus 1 → X32 Output 1
* Bus 2 → X32 Output 2
* Main Left → X32 Output 7
* Main Right → X32 Output 8
Remember that one input channel can feed several destinations simultaneously.
For example, Mic 1 can go to:
* Main L/R
* Bus 1
* Bus 2
Each destination can receive a different amount.
________________


PART 6 — MONITOR MIXES
Step 16 — Understand Bus 1 and Bus 2
Think of each bus as its own completely separate mix.
Main L/R
* Mic 1 — Medium
* Mic 2 — Loud
* Mic 3 — Medium
Bus 1 / Monitor 1
* Mic 1 — Loud
* Mic 2 — Quiet
* Mic 3 — Off
Bus 2 / Monitor 2
* Mic 1 — Quiet
* Mic 2 — Off
* Mic 3 — Loud
All three mixes can exist at the same time.
________________


Step 17 — Build Bus 1 With Sends on Fader
Bus 1 feeds Monitor 1.
Select Bus 1
1. Select the BUS 1–8 fader layer.
2. Locate Bus 1.
3. Press SELECT on Bus 1.
Activate Sends on Fader
Press:
SENDS ON FADER
The button should illuminate.
Normally:
Channel 1 fader = Channel 1 level in Main L/R
With Bus 1 selected and Sends on Fader active:
Channel 1 fader = Channel 1 → Bus 1
Channel 2 fader = Channel 2 → Bus 1
Channel 3 fader = Channel 3 → Bus 1
The faders have temporarily changed jobs.
________________


Step 18 — Create the Bus 1 Mix
While Bus 1 is selected and Sends on Fader is active:
1. Adjust Channel 1.
2. Adjust Channel 2.
3. Adjust Channel 3.
4. Listen to Monitor 1.
For practice, make the differences obvious:
* Mic 1 — Loud
* Mic 2 — Medium
* Mic 3 — Low
________________


Step 19 — Individual Sends vs. Bus Master
These controls do two different things.
Individual Channel Send
Controls:
How much of this specific channel goes into this bus?
Example:
Channel 1 → Bus 1 controls how much Mic 1 is in Monitor 1.
Bus Master
Controls:
How loud is the entire monitor mix?
So:
* One microphone too loud compared with another → adjust its send
* Whole monitor mix too loud → adjust the Bus master
Signal Flow
Channel Sends → Bus 1 → Bus 1 Master → Output 1 → QSC → Monitor 1
________________


Step 20 — Build Bus 2
1. Select Bus 2.
2. Keep Sends on Fader active.
3. Adjust Channels 1–3 again.
The channel faders now represent:
* Channel 1 → Bus 2
* Channel 2 → Bus 2
* Channel 3 → Bus 2
They are no longer showing the Bus 1 mix.
________________


Step 21 — Make Bus 2 Different
For example:
Bus 1
* Mic 1 — Loud
* Mic 2 — Medium
* Mic 3 — Low
Bus 2
* Mic 1 — Low
* Mic 2 — Off
* Mic 3 — Loud
You should clearly hear that the two monitors have different mixes.
________________


Step 22 — Test Bus Independence
Change Mic 3’s level in Bus 2.
Then select Bus 1.
Mic 3 can have a completely different level there.
Changing:
Mic 3 → Bus 1
should not automatically change:
Mic 3 → Bus 2
This independence is the point of separate buses.
________________


Step 23 — Exit Sends on Fader
Press SENDS ON FADER again.
The light should turn off.
Your channel faders now return to their normal jobs.
Troubleshooting Tip
If a fader seems to be controlling the wrong thing:
CHECK SENDS ON FADER FIRST.
________________


Step 24 — Remember This Mental Model
Normal Mode
Channel faders → Main mix
Bus 1 + Sends on Fader
Channel faders → Bus 1 sends
Bus 2 + Sends on Fader
Channel faders → Bus 2 sends
Bus Master
Controls the overall level of that bus.
Basic Workflow
Select destination → Sends on Fader → Build the mix
________________

PART 8 — DANTE MULTITRACK PRACTICE
Once the basic PA is working, Dante can provide a constant 8-track recording for practicing mixing.
The goal is:
Logic Stems → Dante → X32 Channels 9–16
This allows someone to practice faders, buses, EQ, compression, routing, and mixing without needing eight people constantly playing instruments.
________________


Step 30 — Understand the Dante Signal Flow
We will use 8 stems.
The complete path is:
Logic Stem 1 → Output 1 → Dante Ch. 1 → X32 Card 1 → X32 Ch. 9
Logic Stem 2 → Output 2 → Dante Ch. 2 → X32 Card 2 → X32 Ch. 10
Logic Stem 3 → Output 3 → Dante Ch. 3 → X32 Card 3 → X32 Ch. 11
Logic Stem 4 → Output 4 → Dante Ch. 4 → X32 Card 4 → X32 Ch. 12
Logic Stem 5 → Output 5 → Dante Ch. 5 → X32 Card 5 → X32 Ch. 13
Logic Stem 6 → Output 6 → Dante Ch. 6 → X32 Card 6 → X32 Ch. 14
Logic Stem 7 → Output 7 → Dante Ch. 7 → X32 Card 7 → X32 Ch. 15
Logic Stem 8 → Output 8 → Dante Ch. 8 → X32 Card 8 → X32 Ch. 16
________________


Step 31 — Open Dante Controller
On the computer:
1. Open Dante Controller.
2. Find your computer in the list of Dante devices.
3. Find the X32 Compact.
Your computer will be the transmitter.
The X32 Compact will be the receiver.
You do not need to open Dante Virtual Soundcard separately for this exercise.
________________


Step 32 — Connect the Computer to the X32 in Dante Controller
In Dante Controller, use the routing grid.
Route:
* Computer Tx 1 → X32 Rx 1
* Computer Tx 2 → X32 Rx 2
* Computer Tx 3 → X32 Rx 3
* Computer Tx 4 → X32 Rx 4
* Computer Tx 5 → X32 Rx 5
* Computer Tx 6 → X32 Rx 6
* Computer Tx 7 → X32 Rx 7
* Computer Tx 8 → X32 Rx 8
You create these connections by clicking the appropriate intersections in the Dante Controller routing grid.
Make sure each connection is successfully established before continuing.
________________


Step 33 — Set Logic to Dante
Open Logic Pro.
Before creating the eight tracks, set Logic’s audio output device to Dante.
This is important because we want Logic’s Outputs 1–8 to correspond with Dante’s output channels.
Do not simply leave Logic using the computer’s normal built-in output.
________________


Step 34 — Create 8 Tracks in Logic
Create 8 tracks for the eight stems.
When creating them, use Logic’s Ascending option so the tracks are automatically assigned sequential outputs.
The goal is:
* Track 1 → Output 1
* Track 2 → Output 2
* Track 3 → Output 3
* Track 4 → Output 4
* Track 5 → Output 5
* Track 6 → Output 6
* Track 7 → Output 7
* Track 8 → Output 8
Using Ascending is much faster than manually changing the output of every track afterward.
________________


Step 35 — Put One Stem on Each Logic Track
Import the practice stems into Logic.
Place one stem on each track.
For example:
* Track 1 — Drums
* Track 2 — Bass
* Track 3 — Guitar
* Track 4 — Keys
* Track 5 — Lead Vocal
* Track 6 — Background Vocals
* Track 7 — Additional Instrument
* Track 8 — Additional Instrument
The exact instruments can change depending on the practice session.
The important part is:
One stem = one Logic track = one Dante output = one X32 channel.
________________


Step 36 — Route X32 Channels 9–16 to the Dante Card
Now tell the X32 where Channels 9–16 should get their audio.
On the X32:
1. Press ROUTING.
2. Go to the input-routing section.
3. Find the routing block for Channels 9–16.
4. Set that block to:
Card 1–8
Do not change Channels 1–8 to Card.
We want:
X32 Channels 1–8 → Local inputs
and:
X32 Channels 9–16 → Card 1–8
This keeps the wireless microphones available on Channels 1–3 while Dante uses Channels 9–16.
________________


Step 37 — Play the Logic Session
Press Play in Logic.
You should now see separate signal on X32 Channels 9–16.
For example:
* Logic Track 1 → X32 Ch. 9
* Logic Track 2 → X32 Ch. 10
* Logic Track 3 → X32 Ch. 11
* Logic Track 4 → X32 Ch. 12
* Logic Track 5 → X32 Ch. 13
* Logic Track 6 → X32 Ch. 14
* Logic Track 7 → X32 Ch. 15
* Logic Track 8 → X32 Ch. 16
Each stem should have its own X32 channel.
________________


Step 38 — Troubleshoot Dante Using the Signal Path
If you don’t see signal on the X32, don’t randomly change settings.
Follow the signal.
Check in this order:
1. Is Logic playing?
2. Does each Logic track contain audio?
3. Is Logic set to Dante?
4. Are the Logic tracks using Outputs 1–8?
5. Are the outputs ascending correctly?
6. Does Dante Controller show the computer as the transmitter?
7. Does Dante Controller show the X32 as the receiver?
8. Are Tx 1–8 connected to Rx 1–8?
9. Is the X32 routing Channels 9–16 from Card 1–8?
10. Are you looking at X32 Channels 9–16?
Again, the troubleshooting rule is:
Find the point where the signal disappears.
________________


PART 9 — PRACTICE MIXING WITH DANTE
Step 39 — Use the Stems Like a Live Band
Once Logic is playing, Channels 9–16 act like eight separate live inputs.
Practice:
* Setting channel levels
* Muting and unmuting channels
* Panning
* EQ
* Compression
* Gates
* Main L/R mixing
* Bus sends
* Monitor mixes
* Sends on Fader
* Bus master levels
The recording can continuously play while you experiment with the console.
________________


Step 40 — Practice Sending Dante Channels to Bus 1
Select Bus 1 and activate Sends on Fader.
Now Channels 9–16 can be used to create a completely separate Monitor 1 mix.
For example:
* Drums — Loud
* Bass — Medium
* Guitar — Low
* Keys — Off
* Vocal — Loud
Listen to how changing the individual sends changes Monitor 1 without requiring you to change the Main L/R mix.
________________


Step 41 — Practice Sending Dante Channels to Bus 2
Select Bus 2.
With Sends on Fader active, build another mix.
Make it intentionally different from Bus 1.
This is one of the easiest ways to understand monitor mixing because the Logic recording provides a constant signal while you work.
________________

Monitor 2

Dante

Step 43 — Shut Down Safely
When finished:
1. Turn down the system.
2. Turn OFF the M-Audio speakers.
3. Turn OFF the QSC CX702.
4. Then shut down the mixer and other source equipment as appropriate.
Remember:
Speakers and amplifiers ON LAST.
Speakers and amplifiers OFF FIRST.
