## 🎚️ Core skill area

**Live Sound**

## 🌍 Why this matters

The layout changes from one mixer to another. The job does not: accept sources, set usable levels, make a main mix, and send signal to the right destinations.

This activity gives you a small working live-sound system instead of a click-by-click tutorial for one console. You will use an unfamiliar, instructor-approved mixer and explain what each part of the system is doing.

## 🎯 What you'll practice

By the end of the core path, you should be able to:

- Connect a dynamic microphone to a mixer and get it to a main speaker system.
- Connect a line-level playback source to the same main system.
- Use an Aux send to feed a **second real speaker output** independently of the mains.
- Identify mic, line, and speaker-level parts of the system.
- Explain where the preamp and power amp are in each path.
- Keep track of where equipment, cables, and power supplies belong.

## 🗝️ Key terms

**mic level** · **line level** · **speaker level** · **preamp** · **power amp** · **main output** · **aux send** · **aux output** · **powered speaker** · **passive speaker** · XLR · 1/4-inch TRS · 3.5 mm TRS · RCA · SpeakON · gain / trim · channel level · PFL / Solo

## 📍 Logistics

**Time:** One class session is enough to complete the core path. You can continue with another approved mixer or an extension task after that. The goal is transfer, not speed.

**Groups:** Work alone, in a pair, or in a group of three. If you work with other people, take turns operating the mixer.

**Mixer requirement:** Your assigned mixer must have at least one usable Aux send and Aux output. If it does not, use it for comparison or ask the instructor for a different station.

**Likely mixer pool:** compact and medium analog boards such as a Mackie Mix8, Yamaha MG10/2 or MG16/4, Mackie 1604-VLZ family board, or suitable Behringer Xenyx board; Yamaha 01V and Soundcraft Ui16 when assigned. The Mackie Mix5 is useful to compare, but it does not satisfy the Aux requirement. The X32/X32 Compact, CL3, and DM2000 belong in separate, instructor-directed activities unless specifically assigned.

**Output systems:** Your group needs two actual speaker destinations:

1. a main speaker system; and
2. a second powered speaker/monitor system fed from the mixer Aux output.

The second destination may use a properly configured power amp and passive speaker only when the instructor has approved the complete system. Do not invent that path yourself.

## 🧰 Equipment and materials

- [ ] An instructor-approved mixer with at least one Aux send and Aux output
- [ ] A dynamic microphone and appropriate mic cable
- [ ] A laptop or phone playback source and the correct line-input cable/adapter
- [ ] A main speaker system
- [ ] A second powered speaker/monitor system for the Aux output
- [ ] The correct cables for the assigned system
- [ ] The power cable or power supply that belongs with each device
- [ ] A printed worksheet and something to write with

## ⚠️ Safety and handling

- **Identify first.** Before you patch, find the mixer model, its power connection, the main outputs, the Aux output, and the input type expected by each destination.
- **Start quiet.** Pull down relevant channel and master levels before a new connection. Bring each signal path up gradually.
- **Speakers on last, off first.** This reduces startup and shutdown transients through the amplified system.
- **A power supply belongs to a specific device.** Do not exchange wall-wart supplies because a connector happens to fit. Ask before using an unknown supply.
- **A connector does not define the signal.** An XLR or 1/4-inch connection may carry different signals in different contexts. Confirm what the sending and receiving devices expect.
- **A mixer line output does not drive a passive speaker.** A passive speaker needs a power amp between the mixer and the loudspeaker.
- **Do not use 48 V casually.** This activity begins with a dynamic microphone. Ask before using a condenser microphone, active DI, or phantom power.
- **Preserve the room.** Do not change installed X32 speaker routing, use the Film Music cart, overwrite another person's saved scene, or remove equipment that has not been approved for your group.

## 🚀 Core path

### 1. Orient the station before patching

With your group, identify these things on the actual equipment:

- the mixer manufacturer and model;
- its correct power cable or supply;
- one mic input and one line/stereo input;
- the main output;
- one Aux send control and its physical Aux output;
- the main speaker system and the second Aux speaker system;
- where the power amp is in each speaker path.

**Q1 (fill in the blank).** Mixer manufacturer/model: ________________________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's assigned mixer.

**Q2 (fill in the blank).** Main output path, including the amplifier location: mixer main output → ________________________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's actual main output path. A powered speaker contains its amplifier; a passive speaker path requires a separate power amp.

**Q3 (fill in the blank).** Where each borrowed item lives, so you can return it: mixer ________________ · speakers ________________ · cables ________________ · power supplies ________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's own record of where each item came from (room, cabinet, shelf, or cart).

> 🚩 **Checkpoint.** Before you connect a source, your group can point to the mic input, line input, Main output, Aux output, and the amplifier in each speaker path.

### 2. Dynamic microphone to the main speaker system

1. Connect the dynamic microphone to a mic input.
2. Check that the channel, main/master path, and speaker system are ready to receive signal.
3. Starting with levels down, raise the channel gain/trim only as needed for a usable input signal. Then raise the relevant channel and main/output levels carefully.
4. Speak into the mic at a normal presentation volume. Listen for clear output from the main speaker system.

**Q4 (fill in the blank).** Which control on this mixer adjusted the microphone's input gain/trim? ________________________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's own mixer-specific gain/trim control.

> 🚩 **Checkpoint.** A dynamic microphone is audible at a modest, clear level through the main speaker system.

### 3. Line-level playback to the main speaker system

1. Keep the microphone connected.
2. Connect laptop or phone playback to an appropriate line/stereo input. A 3.5 mm headphone output is a line-level playback source, not a microphone.
3. If you have a stereo input and a left/right speaker pair, preserve the left/right connection. If your assigned main system is mono, document that instead of pretending you demonstrated stereo.
4. Bring the playback channel up gradually and confirm it reaches the main speaker system without obvious distortion.
5. Adjust the microphone and playback independently. Confirm that the mixer gives each source its own channel control.

**Q5 (fill in the blank).** Playback source and input path: ________________________________ → ________________________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's actual playback source and line/stereo input path.

> 🚩 **Checkpoint.** Both a microphone and a line-level playback source are audible through the main speaker system, and your group can change one source without changing the other.

### 4. Aux output to a second real speaker

1. Start with the Aux output and second speaker system at a safe low level.
2. Connect the mixer Aux output to the correct line-level input on the second **powered** speaker/monitor system. If the instructor has assigned a separate power amp and passive speaker, follow the verified system path exactly.
3. Choose one source already working in the mains. Turn up that channel's Aux send and then the Aux master/output as needed.
4. Confirm that the second speaker produces signal.
5. Change the selected channel's Aux send. The second speaker should change while the main speaker system remains essentially unchanged.

**Q6 (fill in the blank).** Aux output path, including the amplifier location: mixer Aux output → ________________________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's actual Aux path. A powered monitor contains its amplifier; a passive monitor requires a separate power amp.

**Q7 (fill in the blank).** Which control sent a channel to the second speaker separately from the mains? ________________________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's own mixer-specific Aux/Monitor send control.

**Q8 (fill in the blank).** Label the signal level (mic, line, or speaker) at each point: dynamic mic output ________ · laptop headphone jack ________ · mixer Main or Aux output to a powered speaker ________ · power amp output to a passive speaker ________

✅ **Answer.** mic · line · line · speaker

> 🚩 **Checkpoint.** A real second speaker receives signal from the Aux output, and your group can change that Aux send without changing the main speaker output.

## 🛠️ If something does not work

**No sound from a channel**

- Confirm the source type and input type match.
- Check gain/trim, channel level, mute/on state, main assignment, and main/output level.
- Use PFL/Solo or meters when the mixer offers them.

**Main speaker works; Aux speaker is silent**

- Check the channel's Aux send, Aux master/output, and the physical Aux output.
- Confirm the second speaker has AC power and is receiving line level.
- Confirm you did not connect a mixer line output directly to a passive speaker.

**Playback is distorted or much too loud**

- Verify that playback is using a line/stereo input rather than being treated as a microphone source.
- Lower the playback source and mixer input level, then bring it up again gradually.

**Feedback begins**

- Lower the relevant output or channel immediately.
- Move microphones away from speaker coverage before trying any other adjustment.
- Ask the instructor before making an intentional feedback experiment.

## ✅ Definition of done

- 🚩 I recorded the mixer model, the main output path, and the Aux output path on my worksheet.
- 🚩 I got a dynamic microphone to the main speaker system at a usable level.
- 🚩 I got a line-level playback source to the main speaker system at a usable level.
- 🚩 I sent at least one source from the Aux output to a second real speaker system.
- 🚩 I demonstrated that the Aux send can change the second speaker without changing the mains.
- 🚩 I reset levels, powered down safely, and returned each borrowed item to its proper location.

## ⭐ Explore if time allows

Choose an extension only after the core path works and your worksheet is complete. Work from the most practical options down. Ask before changing a shared system.

1. 🚩 **Repeat the core path on another mixer.** Find the same functions on a different layout. Note what moved or changed names.
2. 🚩 **Use an instrument and DI box.** Route an instrument or other appropriate source through a DI, then into the mixer. Identify whether the DI needs power.
3. 🚩 **Add more inputs.** Build a small main mix with two microphones plus playback or an instrument. Keep each source independently controllable.
4. 🚩 **Build a real stereo main.** Use an assigned left/right powered-speaker pair and verify correct left/right playback.
5. 🚩 **Build a more useful Aux mix.** Put two or more sources into the second speaker and set a balance that differs from the mains.
6. 🚩 **Use PFL/Solo for troubleshooting.** Listen to or meter an input before you send it to the main speakers.
7. 🚩 **Use channel EQ or low cut.** Make a small audible change, then return it to a sensible setting.
8. 🚩 **Make a stereo board feed.** Send the finished mix to an instructor-approved recording/streaming destination and explain why that is different from the Aux monitor path.
9. 🚩 **Try an onboard effect or compressor.** Use it only when the assigned mixer has the feature and you can hear what it changes.
10. 🚩 **Explore multitrack or controlled feedback only with the instructor.** These belong to later live-sound work and need a system that has been prepared for them.

**Q9 (short answer).** Which extensions did you try, and what did you notice? Write down anything that surprised you or that you would do differently on a different mixer.

✅ **Answer.** The student's own notes on the extensions they tried. Full credit for naming at least one extension and one specific observation.

## 🧹 Finish, reset, put away

1. 🚩 Lower the relevant channel, Aux, and main/output levels.
2. 🚩 Turn speakers or power amps off before disconnecting signal cables.
3. 🚩 Disconnect and coil cables properly. Do not leave a pile of cable at the station.
4. 🚩 Return each borrowed cable, speaker, mixer, and power supply to the location you recorded.
5. 🚩 Do not save over someone else's scene or leave an unfamiliar routing change in a shared system.

> 🚩 **Final checkpoint.** The assigned speaker systems are off, levels are down, cables are returned, and the mixer is left in the state your instructor requested.

## 💭 Before you leave

- What was the most useful clue for finding the correct input or output on this mixer?
- Where was the power amplifier in your Main path, and where was it in your Aux path?
- What would you check first if the mains worked but the second speaker did not?
