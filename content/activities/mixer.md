## 🎚️ Core skill area

**Live Sound**

## 🌍 Why this matters

Every mixer is laid out a little differently, and a lot of them are laid out very differently — an analog board, a digital board with a touchscreen, a rack-mounted interface pretending to be a mixer. Nobody is going to hand you a click-by-click guide for the specific board you'll be standing in front of on the day it actually matters.

The real skill isn't memorizing one board. It's being self-motivated enough to sit down at a mixer you've never touched and figure out how to get sound in, get sound out, and not blow anything up on the way. This activity is built around that: three things every board can do, plus a long menu of everything else a mixer might do, sorted by how hard it usually is rather than by what kind of task it is.

## 🎯 What you'll practice

By the end of this activity, you should be able to:

- Get a real microphone signal through an unfamiliar mixer and out a PA speaker, safely.
- Get a stereo playback source through the same system.
- Build a monitor send that's separately controllable from the main mix.
- Recognize that boards differ in what they offer — not every mixer has a compressor, a digital EQ, or spare aux buses — and adapt instead of assuming your last board's layout.

## 🗝️ Key terms

**channel strip** · **gain / trim** · **fader** · **aux send** · **aux bus** · **monitor send (wedge)** · pan · pre-fader vs. post-fader · **48V phantom power** · **DI (Direct Input) box** · active vs. passive · **EQ** · low cut / high-pass · notch filter · **compressor** · **reverb** · **board feed** · multitrack recording

## 📍 Logistics

**Estimated time:** 30–60 minutes for the three essentials below. Open-ended past that — the more of the bonus menu you attempt, the longer it runs. You don't have to do it all in one sitting.

**Where:** Any mixer you have approved access to. Known options on campus include the **Behringer X32 Compact** in OH B01 and the **Yamaha CL3** in OH 207 — see **Technical verification** for which rooms/boards students can actually use unsupervised. A plain analog board works fine for the three essentials and most of the bonus menu; a few bonus tasks (notably **Ring out the room**) specifically need a board with digital EQ on the main output.

**Access:** In class / on campus. Supervision requirements vary by room and board — see Technical verification.

**Group size:** Minimum 1, ideal 2, maximum 3. This activity is built to reward being self-motivated on gear you've never touched, so solo is genuinely fine. In a pair or trio, rotate who's actually driving the board so everyone gets hands-on time.

## 🧰 Equipment and materials

- [ ] A mixing board — analog or digital, whatever you have approved access to
- [ ] At least 1 microphone (a condenser needs 48V phantom power — check before you plug it in)
- [ ] At least 1 PA speaker (mains), powered or driven by an amp
- [ ] Mic and speaker cables matched to your gear
- [ ] A stereo playback source (phone or laptop, 3.5mm or Bluetooth)
- [ ] Your OneDrive activity journal for this activity, open before you start
- [ ] A way to record short proof videos (your phone is fine)
- [ ] Only if you're attempting that specific bonus task: a second mic, a DI box, a second/monitor speaker, an audio interface plus a DAW with multitrack stems, or a separate 2-track recorder

## ⚠️ Safety and handling — read this before you touch anything

- 🔌 **Speakers ON last, OFF first.** Every time, no exceptions. Get everything else connected and set before you power the PA on; kill the PA before you start unplugging anything else.
- 🔋 **Confirm before engaging 48V.** It's for condensers and active DI boxes only. Turn it on before you plug the mic in, not after, and don't leave it on for gear that doesn't want it.
- 🎚️ **Bring new signal paths up from the bottom, not down from full.** Start every new fader or gain knob low and raise it, rather than starting hot and pulling back.
- 🚨 **Feedback you're causing on purpose still needs a low starting gain and a hand ready to pull it down instantly.** A runaway feedback loop at full volume is a hearing-safety issue, not just an annoyance — see the Controlled Feedback bonus task below for how to do this safely.
- 🗂️ **If the board has a saved scene or preset that isn't yours, don't overwrite it without asking.** Some boards remember what the last person left; you may not know what you're erasing.

## 🎙️ Part 1 — The three essentials

Whatever board you end up on, these three are required. The bonus menu below assumes you already have a working mic and a working speaker, so it isn't worth opening until these are done.

**1. Single mic → PA.** Connect one microphone to the board and get it out through at least one PA speaker.

- If it's a condenser, turn on 48V before you plug it in, not after — check your board's manual or channel labeling for where that switch actually lives, since it's rarely in the same place twice.
- Bring the fader up from the bottom.
- Prove it: record yourself talking into the mic and hearing it come out the PA. Upload the video to your OneDrive journal.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** You can hear your own voice, at a normal talking volume, coming out of the PA speaker — not headphones, not the board's own monitor jack.

**Q1 (fill in the blank).** Write down the mixer brand and model you used today, so your later journal entries make sense: ______________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's own — whatever mixer they actually used.

**2. Stereo playback → PA.** Connect a stereo source — laptop, phone, whatever you have — to the board and route it to the same PA.

- Prove it: play a "stereo test" track (searchable on YouTube — one that calls out or hard-pans left vs. right) and record it working. If your PA is a single mono speaker, say so in your journal instead of faking a stereo demo.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** You can clearly tell left from right coming out of the speakers — or you've noted in your journal that your PA can't show that.

**Q2 (multiple choice).** You plug a laptop's stereo headphone output straight into two mic-level channels on the board. What usually happens?

- a) Nothing changes — mic and line level are the same thing
- ✅ b) It's much too loud or distorted, because mic-level inputs expect a far weaker signal than line-level playback
- c) The channels stay silent no matter what you do
- d) The board automatically pans it correctly

**3. Monitor send.** Send a signal you already have working to a second speaker, positioned as a monitor rather than a main.

- Prove it: record a video where you can independently confirm the monitor is carrying signal — for example, turning the monitor send up and down while the mains stay put.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** You can raise and lower what the monitor hears without changing what the mains are doing.

**Q3 (fill in the blank).** Name the specific control — fader, knob, or menu item — you used to send signal to the monitor separately from the mains: ______________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's own — whichever control on their specific board.

## 🛠️ Troubleshooting

**IF you get no sound at all:**
→ Is the channel muted? Fader at the bottom? Gain/trim at zero?
→ Is the main output/master fader actually up?
→ Right cable and input type for that source — XLR mic input vs. 1/4" line vs. 3.5mm?
→ Condenser mic and 48V isn't on?

**IF you get feedback you didn't intend:**
→ Pull the fader down immediately. Don't try to EQ your way out of it in the moment.
→ Check mic position relative to the speakers — mains almost always go in front of performers, not behind them or beside a monitor aimed at the mic.
→ Lower gain/trim before bringing the fader back up.

**Q4 (multiple choice).** You plug in an active DI box and get nothing. Most likely cause?

- a) DI boxes don't work with mixers
- ✅ b) It needs 48V and phantom power isn't on
- c) The cable is too long
- d) The DI box is set to the wrong sample rate

**IF a task below mentions a feature your board "doesn't have":**
→ That's expected, not a bug in these instructions. Analog boards especially may have no compressor, no built-in reverb, and only basic EQ. Note it in your journal and move to a different bonus task instead of forcing it.

## ✅ Definition of done

- 🚩 You got one microphone through the board and out a PA speaker, with a proof video in your OneDrive journal.
- 🚩 You got a stereo source playing back through the same system with left and right audibly distinct (or you noted a mono-PA exception), proof video included.
- 🚩 You built a monitor send that changes independently of the mains and proved it on video.
- 🚩 Your journal names the exact mixer brand and model you used.

Everything under **Bonus** below is optional. Attempt as many as you like, in any order, and log each the same way — a short video plus a line in your journal naming the task and which board feature you used.

## ⭐ Bonus / if time allows — pick your own path

- 🟢 **More real mics.** Add a second (or third) microphone and get them all into the mains at once. Make sure at least one of them needs 48V, so you're forced to find that control on this board too. Prove it: a video with multiple live mics audible together.

- 🟢 **Use a DI (Direct Input) box.** Capture a guitar, bass, or someone's phone/aux cable through a DI box instead of a mic. Passive DIs need no power; active ones need 48V, same as a condenser. Prove it: a video showing the signal path from instrument/aux → DI → mixer → PA.

- 🟦 **Controlled feedback.** Deliberately create feedback — at a volume you can kill instantly — then figure out roughly which frequency was ringing. Prove it: a video showing the feedback starting and you killing it.

  🚩 **Checkpoint.** You caused feedback on purpose, at a volume you could immediately pull back down, and you can say roughly what frequency it was.

**Q5 (fill in the blank).** Roughly which frequency or frequency range fed back for you? ______________________

✅ **Answer.** The student's own — whatever they actually found on their specific board, mic, and speaker combination.

- 🟢 **EQ on a channel.** Try a low cut on every channel except your bass or other line-level input, and sweep a band on one channel to hear the tone change. Prove it: a video demonstrating the audible difference with the EQ in vs. bypassed.

- 🟢 **Board feed recording.** Record straight off the board's own 2-track or board-feed output onto a separate recorder, rather than recording per channel. Prove it: play the recording back afterward on video.

- 🟦 **Multitrack PA (imaginary mics, real music).** Use a computer with a DAW and an audio interface that has multiple discrete outputs. Load real multitrack stems and assign one output per track into the mixer, then mix it like a live band. Prove it: a video of the "imaginary band" coming out your PA.

- ⬥ **Custom monitor mixes.** Build a monitor mix that's actually different from the main mix — an aux bus you control independently, not just the mains routed to a second speaker (that's the monitor-send essential above; this is the harder, more common-in-practice version).

  🚩 **Checkpoint.** You can change what the monitor hears without that change showing up in the mains at all — genuinely independent, not the same signal at two volumes.

- ⁉️ **Compressor.** Only if your board has one. Demonstrate something dynamic — whisper "quiet," then shout "LOUD" — first with the compressor off, then again with it on.

**Q6 (multiple choice).** To actually prove a compressor is doing something, not just that you turned a knob, the video needs to show:

  - a) Only the loud scream, close up on the meter
  - ✅ b) The same whisper-then-scream performance twice — once with the compressor off, once with it on — so there's something to compare
  - c) The compressor's model number on camera
  - d) The performance done as quietly as possible the whole time

- ⬥ **Reverb.** Set up an aux bus as a reverb send/return (not an insert). Prove it: a video of something dry, then the same thing with reverb added.

- ⁉️ **Multitrack recording.** Capture every channel separately, not just a 2-track board feed. How hard this is depends entirely on the board — some make it trivial with a built-in multitrack recorder or USB interface, some make it very difficult. Prove it: a video demonstrating it, plus the actual multitrack files.

- ⬥⬥ **Ring out the room.** Needs a board with a digital parametric EQ on the main output — the X32 Compact and the CL3 both qualify. Deliberately push a mic toward feedback, then apply one or more narrow notch filters at the ringing frequency so the same gain no longer feeds back.

  🚩 **Checkpoint.** You can push the gain back to about where it was feeding back before, and it no longer feeds back.

**Q7 (short answer).** After you apply the notch filter at the feeding-back frequency, what should change when you push the gain back up to the same level as before?

✅ **Answer.** It shouldn't feed back at that level anymore — the notch filter cut the specific frequency the room/mic/speaker combination was ringing at, so there's less gain available at that frequency before feedback starts again.

## 🧹 Finish, reset, put away

1. Turn speakers OFF first, always — before you start unplugging anything else.
2. Mute or pull down every channel you used.
3. Undo anything you changed that wasn't yours to change. If you're not sure a board setting is fine to leave altered, ask before you leave it that way.
4. Return any borrowed mics, cables, DI boxes, or interfaces to where you found them.
5. Confirm your OneDrive journal has a video and a line for every task you attempted today.

🚩 **Final checkpoint.** Before you walk away: are the speakers off, and is the board back the way you found it (or in a state you were told is fine to leave)?
