> 🧪 **Status: Pilot.** This activity is new, and its own instructions are being tested along
> with everything else. If a step here confuses you, that's a finding too. Report it the same way.

## 🌍 Why this matters

**The repo** is the set of files behind this course site: every activity, checklist, and
worksheet you use. Most of it was drafted with AI help from the instructor's notes, then
corrected. Some steps are confirmed on the real gear. Others have never been tested.

Your job: follow one small section for real, notice exactly where it stops matching reality, and
help leave a better version for the next person. That's **testing**, not polishing. A page that
reads nicely but names the wrong button is worse than an ugly one that's right.

## 🛣️ Two ways to do it

Both start the same way: pick one small section and **actually try it**. They differ in what you
hand in.

### 🗣️🎙️ Talk it out

No AI, no files, no Markdown. After you test, **dictate one paragraph of raw feedback**. Phone
voice typing is fine. Say:

- which page and section, and where you tried it (room and gear, or at home on paper)
- what you expected to happen, and what actually happened
- exactly where you got stuck or confused, and what you'd change

Raw is fine. Specific beats polished: "Step 4 says hold the switch; it took about 2 seconds
before the F8 turned on" is worth more than "step 4 was confusing."

### 🧹🤖🍝 Use AI to clean AI slop

Copy the **starter + source** below (one tap) and paste it into an AI tool. PantherAI works on
your phone. The starter sets the
AI up to ask you short questions about what happened, then write a **revised version of the
page** plus a short **change and test report**. You check every claim it makes against what you
actually saw.

## 🗝️ Key terms

**activity** · **checklist** · **reference** · **observation** · **suggestion** ·
**verification status** · **blocker** · revision · AI-use disclosure

## 📚 Three kinds of document

An **activity** teaches: what to practice, a numbered procedure, and what success looks like. A
**checklist** runs a real job: short ordered actions and checks, no lessons. **Reset procedures**
are checklists too. A **reference** explains: equipment facts, diagrams, settings,
troubleshooting. A good activity *links to* its checklist and references instead of copying them.

## 🏷️ Verification status

If you revise steps, mark every step you touch with one of these:

| Mark | Means |
|---|---|
| ✅ Verified | You did it today, on the real gear or the named simulator, and it worked as written. |
| 📝 Proposed | You changed it and haven't re-tested the changed version yet. |
| ❓ Unverified | It was in the source, and you didn't get to test it. |
| ⛔ Blocked | You couldn't test it, and you say why (no access, gear missing, needs the instructor). |

## 📍 Logistics

**Time:** 30–90 minutes for one section. Pick something small. One section you really tested
beats a whole page you skimmed.

| Where | What you can do there |
|---|---|
| 🏠 At home | Simulators (Salmon, mixer-lab, camera-lab), and desk checks: read a page, find gaps and contradictions. Physical steps stay ❓ Unverified until someone tries them on the gear. |
| 🏫 On campus, during class | Hands-on testing with the instructor in the building. |
| 🔑 On campus, outside class | Only where the page you picked **already** says you can work outside class. "Confirm with instructor" means ask first. |

Being on campus doesn't mean you can get into a room or use its gear. Each page's own **Access**
and **Supervision** lines still apply.

**Group size:** Solo, or 2–3 people. In a group, everyone should get hands on the gear at some
point.

## ⚠️ Ground rules — read before you start

- 🚫 **Never invent** a result, a setting, a port label, a menu path, an access permission, or a
  peer's feedback. "I don't know yet" is a complete, honest answer. Write it down.
- 🔒 **No improvised equipment changes.** If a step needs a setting the page says not to touch, or
  a room you don't have access to, stop and say so. A finished-looking result isn't worth a
  changed preamp before a recital.
- 🧩 **Your feedback or revision is a proposal.** The instructor decides what becomes the approved
  procedure.
- 🙈 **Keep private things out:** no names, door codes, passwords, stream keys, or account details
  in anything that might end up on this site.
- 🧱 **Keep the working parts.** Don't rewrite steps that already work. Keep room names, equipment
  names, and labels exactly as they appear on the gear.

## 🧭 Procedure

### 1. Choose one thing to test

Pick **one** section from the starter menu below, or one the instructor assigns, and say exactly
what you'll test: "Part 2 of Basic Stereo Recording, the gain-staging drill, on the stereo kit in
class." Not "the stereo page." Check the location labels first: 🏠 items work anywhere; hands-on
items need their room and gear.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** You've written down one section, the page it's on, and where you'll test it.

**Q1 (multiple choice).** A page lists every button on the Zoom F8's front panel, what each one
does, and why, with a diagram, but gives no order to do anything in. Which type of document is
it?

- a) Activity
- b) Checklist
- ✅ c) Reference

### 2. Note the starting version

Write down the page's URL and its **revised** date (top of the page or on its worksheet). On the
🧹🤖🍝 route, the **starter + source** download records both for you.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** You know exactly which version of the page you started from.

### 3. Say what success looks like

Read the section through once without touching anything. Note what it says *not* to do, and what
access it needs. Then finish this sentence: *if this works, we'll see / hear …*

🚩 **Checkpoint.** You've written what a successful run should look or sound like, before you
start.

### 4. Do it, exactly as written

Follow the steps as written, not as you think they meant. At every point where the instructions
are **missing, confusing, contradictory, outdated, or don't match the equipment**, note the exact
step, what you expected, and what actually happened. If it's allowed, take a photo of a label or
screen, after checking the whole frame for private info. Keep going if it's safe to; otherwise
stop and note what's blocked.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** Every problem you hit is written down with the exact step and what you
observed.

**Q2 (multiple choice).** Which of these is an **observation**, not a suggestion?

- a) "Step 4 should say to hold the switch longer."
- ✅ b) "We held the power switch for 1 second and the F8 stayed off; at about 2 seconds it turned on."
- c) "The F8 probably needs a firmware update."
- d) "The AI said this step is confusing."

### 5. Talk it out, or clean it up

**🗣️🎙️ Talk it out:** dictate your paragraph of raw feedback now, while it's fresh (see **Two
ways to do it**).

**🧹🤖🍝 Use AI to clean AI slop:** paste the starter + source into your AI tool. If you picked a
simulator or another site, fill in its blanks and paste that part's on-screen text first. Then
answer the AI's questions about what happened. Keep the steps that worked. Fix only what your test showed. Every
changed step gets marked 📝 Proposed, and observations stay separate from suggestions.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** You have either a feedback paragraph or a complete revised page with every
changed step marked.

**Q3 (fill in the blank).** You rewrote step 6 but haven't tried the new version yet. Its
verification status is ______.

✅ **Answer.** Proposed (📝). It only becomes Verified once someone has re-run the changed step
and it worked.

### 6. Re-test what you changed

On the 🧹🤖🍝 route, run just the steps you changed, using your revision, and update each mark.
If someone who didn't write it can follow your revision, even better. Note where they paused. If
nobody could, say so. Never write down a test or a tester that didn't happen.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** Every changed step is ✅ Verified or has a written reason it isn't.

### 7. Submit

Submit on Canvas: your paragraph (🗣️🎙️), **or** the revised page plus the AI's change and test
report, including your AI-use disclosure (🧹🤖🍝). Didn't finish? Submit what you have, with
what blocked you and the next useful step. An honest account of a blocked test is a real
contribution.

**Want public credit?** Add your **GitHub username** to your submission. If your improvement
makes it onto the site, that page can list you as a contributor. That's opt-in, and it only ever
shows the username, never your real name.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** Your feedback or revision is submitted, or saved and ready to submit.

### 8. Reset the space

Put every piece of gear back the way the page's own reset section says (or the way you found
it). Turn off what you turned on, and nothing else.

🚩 **Checkpoint.** The room is ready for the next person.

## 🆘 Stuck?

1. **Write down the exact obstacle**: the step, what you saw, what you expected. That's a finding,
   not a failure.
2. **Check a page that's already linked** from the one you're testing.
3. **Ask a classmate.**
4. **Ask the instructor** when it needs a decision, an access permission, or a setting you've been
   told not to touch.

**Gear or room unavailable?** Switch to a 🏠 item from the starter menu, or report the blocked
test as your finding. Never make up results.

## ✅ Definition of done

- 🚩 One small section was really tested, on the gear, in a simulator, or as a desk check that says so.
- 🚩 Problems are written down with the exact step, what you expected, and what you observed.
- 🚩 Your feedback paragraph or marked-up revision is submitted or ready to submit.
- 🚩 The equipment and room are reset.

## 💭 Before you leave

- The one step most likely to trip up the next person, and why.
- One thing about *this* page you'd change.
