## ⏳ What this is

Every gig runs the same arc, whatever the room: before, during, after, then post-production and
the archive. The gear changes from gig to gig; the responsibilities don't. This page is the map.
On the night, follow the checklist for that gig, not this page.

## 🗝️ Key terms

**gig** · **service gig** · **archival** · **broadcast** · **call time** · **downbeat** ·
**primary wide** · **gig folder** · **r / e / s** · **Post drive** · **Backups drive** ·
**Triple Triple Check** · **complete concert file**

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Gig** | Any event that needs recording engineers. |
| **Service gig** | A gig that isn't recorded: tech support, helping in a tech class. ❓ |
| **Archival** | A standard recording: single camera, not for public release. ❓ |
| **Broadcast** | A special case: multiple cameras, meant for public release. ❓ |
| **Call time** | When you're due to arrive. ❓ The old manual also makes it when your timesheet starts. |
| **Downbeat** | When the event is scheduled to start. |
| **Primary wide** | The one wide shot of the whole stage that every gig has. |
| **Gig folder** | `YYMMDD Full Event Name`, for example `260914 The Chapman Orchestra`. |
| **r / e / s** | Inside the gig folder: **r**aw source files, **e**dit (post-production) files, **s**aved final deliverables. An optional **a** holds special archival files. |
| **Post drive, Backups drive** | The two places every recording is copied to. "Work drive" means the Post drive. |
| **Triple Triple Check** | The proof, before you wipe a card, that the files are safe on both drives. |
| **Complete concert file** | The whole event in one unedited file, also called **top and tail**. ❓ |

## 📅 1. Before the gig

- Know the venue, the call time, the concert, and which services it needs.
- Gather and check the gear before you leave.
- Dress for it: closed-toed shoes and no logos on your shirt; all black at Musco.
- Arrive fed and caffeinated.
- ❓ Gigs are assigned from the availability you've given. Confirm an assignment within 72 hours,
  or find your own replacement.

## 🎬 2. The day of the gig

- **Be early.** ❓ The old manual's version: "If you're on time, you're already fifteen minutes
  late." Its call location was BH 209.
- ❓ **Everything is ready before the doors open**, usually 15–30 minutes before the downbeat.
  Confirm the time for each gig.
- **A backup for everything.** Current checklists build one in (the Salmon Gig Checklist's H6 and
  AX100). ❓ The old manual made it a rule for every gig: a backup audio recorder and a backup
  camera.
- **Check, then double-check, batteries and the time left on every card.**
- **Protect the primary wide.** ❓ The old manual's rules: leave room for bows; reframe between
  pieces, never in the last seconds before the music starts, and not during the music unless
  there's unexpected choreography; it must never run out of battery or card space.
- **Ask early** about anything that depends on someone else: house staff, lighting, performers,
  or getting into the room. It can't be fixed at the last minute.
- **At intermission, leave the recorders running** unless a card or drive is actually about to
  run out of space.
- **Protect the performance:** stay quiet, stay organized, and solve problems without disrupting
  the event.

## 💾 3. After the gig

- Stop and power down what you used, and return the gear ready for the next engineer. Some rooms
  have a reset procedure for this.
- **Transfer the media: one source, two destinations.** Copy everything from each card or SSD to
  the **Post** drive and the **Backups** drive, into the gig folder's `r [abbr]` folder, in a
  subfolder named for where it came from (`From Mus128_1842`, `From F8_03`).
- Put a copy of the **program** on the drives with the files.
- **Triple Triple Check before you wipe anything:** the approximate size, the last three digits
  of the exact byte count, and the location (the source plus both drives) all match. If you're
  99% sure, don't delete; ask. Then empty the trash and confirm the space came back.
- Someone else started a transfer and had to leave? Finish it.
- ❓ The lead engineer confirms the transfer is complete within 48 hours.

The step-by-step version is in the [Salmon Gig Checklist](../../salmon-gig-checklist/)'s **Data
transfer** section.

## ✂️ 4. Post-production and delivery

When you're assigned post: a fast turnaround, and clear communication. **Salmon: Basic A/V
Capture** lists what a real gig's post involves: transcoding, a Google Drive upload, an email
confirming completion, Panopto, and a task board, with deadlines of one week and three weeks.

❓ What the old manual and its Post QC checklist add:

- **Deadlines:** single-camera audio synced to video within 1 week; all post within 3 weeks.
- **Software:** Logic for audio and Final Cut for video, unless approved otherwise in advance.
- **Deliverables:** a **complete concert file** (4K video plus a WAV), and **individual pieces**
  (1080p video files and WAV files, one per piece).
- **Audio:** always stereo, never summed to mono; WAV at 48 kHz, 16- or 24-bit; a limiter; reverb
  usually not (Salmon especially).
- **Video:** H.264, with AAC audio.
- **The complete concert file is never edited.** It keeps everything from the walk-on to the
  applause, plus space at the end. It lets the performer watch the whole event in one sitting,
  and it means a later re-edit can't lose anything: an encore cut too soon is otherwise gone.
- **Delivery:** only the designated supervisor notifies the client. Every delivery says that
  archival recordings are for private use, and can't be posted publicly without written
  permission from every performer and composer.

## 🗄️ 5. The archive

- The `s` folder holds the final deliverables, kept for good.
- ❓ Deliverables go to the department's recording archive (SharePoint in the old manual; it says
  Panopto is no longer used).
- ❓ Raw files come off the hard drives only after the client confirms they received the recording
  and the upload is safe, and only the instructor deletes them.

**Q1 (multiple choice).** A gig is in Salmon Recital Hall. What does that tell you about the
service you're providing?

- a) It's a recording, nothing else
- b) It's FOH, nothing else
- ✅ c) Not enough: Salmon can mean recording, livestreaming, FOH, or a combination
- d) It's always a livestream

**Q2 (fill in the blank).** Data transfer copies one source to ______ destinations: the
________ drive and the ________ drive.

✅ **Answer.** Two: the Post drive and the Backups drive. A hard drive can fail at any time.

**Q3 (short answer).** Inside a gig folder, what goes in **r**, **e**, and **s**?

✅ **Answer.** **r**: raw source recordings. **e**: edit and post-production files. **s**: saved
final deliverables. (An optional **a** holds special archival files.)

**Q4 (multiple choice).** It's intermission and every recorder is running. What do you do?

- a) Pause everything the moment the lights come up
- ✅ b) Leave everything recording, unless a card or drive is actually about to run out of space
- c) Power everything off to save battery
- d) Swap in fresh cards just in case
