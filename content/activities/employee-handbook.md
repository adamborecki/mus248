## 🎧 What this is

How the job works if you're a **student recording engineer**: a student employee who records,
runs, and supports the music department's gigs. The gigs themselves are on the **Gigs 2026–27**
sheet (linked on the front page). How a single recording goes from start to finish is in
[Lifespan of a Recording](../../recording-lifespan/).

## 🗝️ Key terms

**recording engineer** · **post-production engineer** · **head engineer** · **lead engineer** ·
**call time** · **setup time** · **downbeat** · **end time** · **status code** · **on call**

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Recording engineer** | A student employee who works gigs case by case, as they come up. Their post-production is single-camera audio/video sync and editing only. |
| **Post-production engineer** | A student employee who averages 10–15 hours a week, with extra duties in data management and archiving. ❓ |
| **Head engineer** | The senior student engineer: your first stop for questions, and (with the instructor) the person who assigns gigs. |
| **Lead engineer** | "Engineer 1" on a gig: the primary engineer, who owns the data transfer. |
| **Gig** / **service gig** | Any event that needs engineers / one that isn't recorded (tech support, a class assistant shift). |

## 🤝 Professionalism and work ethic

You're expected to have a professional attitude. For example:

- Arrive early. (In the performing arts, "on time" is already fifteen minutes late.)
- No phone on the job, within reason.
- Don't sleep on the job.
- Anticipate problems, and report any you see.
- Show concern and actively try to help when you can.
- Go above and beyond the bare minimum.
- **If something is broken, don't put it back.** Report it.

## 💚 Health and well-being

You're student employees, and the first word of that is **student**. If things are great, that's
great. If they aren't, say so, and let's troubleshoot it.

## 🚨 Harassment, discrimination, and retaliation

Everyone contributes to a safe work environment. If you've seen or experienced harassment or
discrimination, however small it seems, **say something**: to the instructor, or directly to
Chapman HR. It's taken seriously, the process is confidential, and there are legal and policy
protections against retaliation.

## 📅 Scheduling

> ❓ **The old manual ran everything through Outlook Calendar.** Gigs are now listed on the
> **Gigs 2026–27** sheet, so some of this has probably changed. It's kept here until it's
> reviewed.

**Keep your calendar accurate, always.** Gigs are assigned from the availability you give. Put
everything on it: classes, rehearsals, other jobs, and post-production hours. Sync it to your
phone and computer. An **all-day or multi-day** event (out of town, for example) has to be set to
**Busy** by hand.

**Assignments.** The instructor or the head engineer assigns gigs. Each assignment gives the gig's
title and ID, date and location, its times (call, setup, downbeat, end), notes, gig type, release
type, who receives the recording, the services (audio, video, PA or live sound, other), and the
engineers.

- **Accept** an assignment as soon as you can, and no later than three days after it's sent.
  Accepting confirms you'll be there.
- **Can't do it?** Decline, then **find your own replacement within 72 hours**, and email the Music
  Recordings account `[address — ask instructor]` once it's confirmed. If you can't find one, you
  work the gig.
- Arriving late or leaving early is rare, and needs the instructor's approval **in writing**.
- You won't be assigned two gigs at the same time.
- Listed as an **optional attendee**? You're **on call** or the **backup engineer**.

### ⏱️ The times on a gig

| Time | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Call time** | When you're due to arrive (BH 209 in the old manual ❓), and when you clock in. |
| **Setup time** | When the venue is free for setup. In Salmon (the built-in gear) it's the same as the call time; for Musco or portable gigs, the call is often an hour or two earlier. |
| **Downbeat** | When the event is scheduled to start: the listed start time for a concert. |
| **End time** | The estimated end: the concert is over, gear is back, and the data transfer has started. |
| **Data transfer time** | ❓ The lead engineer comes back within 48 hours to finish the data transfer. |

### 🏷️ Status codes in a gig's title

| Code | Means |
|---|---|
| `[?]` | Potential or unconfirmed gig: details still to be confirmed. |
| `[-]` | Engineers assigned, awaiting confirmation: confirm your availability. |
| `[>]` | Engineers assigned and confirmed. If your name is in the title, you're on it. |
| `[X]` | Canceled: the gig won't happen. |

### 🧑‍💼 Lead and post engineer

- **Lead engineer** (Engineer 1): starts the data transfer, and follows up within 48 hours
  (files confirmed transferred, SD cards cleaned). ❓
- **Post engineer** (the name in bold in the gig description; sometimes the lead, sometimes not):
  ❓ delivers the wide single-camera audio and video within **1 week**, and the edited, separated
  single-camera audio and video within **3 weeks**. Multicam edits are assigned case by case.

### 📋 Requirements

- ❓ **At least 5 gigs per semester**, with you as the primary engineer on at least 3. Missing the
  minimum may mean losing the job.
- **Reasonable availability, including nights and weekends.** Consistent scheduling problems may
  mean losing the job.
- ❓ A **weekly recording engineer meeting**.

### 📟 On call (a draft policy, never made official)

- **1.5 hours before the downbeat:** text the primary engineer to confirm they're on their way.
- **1 hour before:** either be able to get to campus if they don't answer, or be on the phone to
  confirm their setup ("how much time is left on the SSD recorder?").
- **During the show:** be available, though you probably won't be needed.
- **After the show:** be available to confirm the post-production steps were done right.

A **backup engineer** (also a draft) is there for emergencies.

## 🎁 Perks

- **Free recital recording** for music majors on staff. It doesn't include post-production: you
  edit your own recital (with the department's drives, computers, and software), and those hours
  don't go on your timesheet.
- **Borrowing gear for your own projects**, with permission **in writing, in advance**. (The answer
  is almost always yes.)
