## 2. The Standard Gear Kit: A Refresher

### Any venue, same kit

- ==Memorial==, ==Fish==, or anywhere else we bring the gear: the **Standard Gear Kit**
- Two **==AX100s==**, a stereo pair into the **==F8==**, an **==H4n==** as backup
- [Standard Gear Kit Checklist](../../standard-gear-kit-checklist/)

**Q6 (multiple choice).** Which gigs use the Standard Gear Kit?

- ✅ a) Memorial, Fish, or anywhere else we bring the gear
- b) Only Salmon
- c) Only Musco

✅ **Answer.** Any venue where we bring the gear: the same kit, the same setup.

### Ask for concert lighting

- You **must** ask for ==concert lighting==: the ==brightest== cue of the show
- Set the exposure while looking at the ==stage== **with concert lighting on**, never with the
  pre-show lights
- Must. Must. Must.

**Q7 (fill in the blank).** Set the cameras' exposure while looking at the stage with ==concert lighting== on.

✅ **Answer.** You must ask for concert lighting, the brightest cue of the show. The pre-show lights are much darker.

### Cameras: two AX100s, both wide

- Both **==static==**, both **==wide==**
- Different ==angles== are fine, but both wide
- One of them is the ==backup==
- Both on the **same** ==white balance==

**Q8 (multiple choice).** How are the two AX100s framed on a Standard Gear Kit gig?

- a) One wide, one following the soloists
- b) Both on close-ups of the performers
- ✅ c) Both static and wide; one of them is the backup

✅ **Answer.** Both static, both wide. Different angles are fine. Both on the same white balance.

### Audio: the F8

- Main pencil mics in **==ORTF==** or **==NOS==** → Inputs **1–2**
- **==Dual Channel== Recording** copies 1–2 to **5–6**, with the gain **==lower==** (≈ 42 dB on 1–2, ≈ 30 dB on 5–6)
- Inputs **3–4** and **7–8**: ==boundary mics== and/or a ==board feed==

**Q9 (multiple choice).** What does Dual Channel Recording do with the main pair on Inputs 1–2?

- ✅ a) Copies them to 5–6, with the gain lower
- b) Copies them to 5–6, with the same gain
- c) Copies them to 3–4, with the gain higher

✅ **Answer.** Dual Channel copies 1–2 to 5–6 at a lower gain (about 42 dB on 1–2, about 30 dB on 5–6), a safety copy in case of a loud peak.

**Q10 (multiple choice).** Which F8 inputs are free for boundary mics or a board feed?

- a) 1–2
- ✅ b) 3–4 and 7–8
- c) 5–6

✅ **Answer.** 1–2 are the main pair and 5–6 are its Dual Channel copies, so 3–4 and 7–8 are for boundary mics and/or a board feed.

### Backup: the H4n

- The H4n is the **==backup==** recording
- Gain set ==conservatively==: a quiet backup beats a ==clipped== one

**Q11 (multiple choice).** How is the H4n's gain set?

- ✅ a) Conservatively: a quiet backup beats a clipped one
- b) As hot as possible, just under clipping
- c) On auto

✅ **Answer.** The H4n is the backup. Set it conservatively: a quiet backup can be turned up; a clipped one can't be fixed.

### Vocal Jazz and CME

| | Vocal Jazz | CME |
|---|---|---|
| Who | Singers only | A band, with lead and backing vocals |
| Mics | Thirteen ==wireless== handhelds | Five wireless, plus the band on a ==sub snake== |
| Mains | The hall's speakers | Its own, **==downstage== of the band** |
| Wedges | Three wedges, **one** mix | Three wedges, **three** ==mixes== |

- Same X32, same S32, same routing. What changes is the **==outputs==**

**Q13 (multiple choice).** Vocal Jazz and CME use the same X32, S32, and routing. What changes between them?

- ✅ a) The outputs: the mains and the wedges
- b) The input routing
- c) The console

✅ **Answer.** Same rig. CME brings its own mains downstage and runs three wedge mixes; Vocal Jazz uses the hall's speakers and one wedge mix.

### Using AES50

- Mics come **in** at the ==X32==
- Everything goes **out** through the **==S32==**, the ==stage box==
- One Cat5e between them: **==AES50==**
- No AES50 link: every ==meter== moves, **every speaker is silent**

**Q14 (multiple choice).** Every meter on the X32 is moving, but nothing comes out of any speaker in the hall. What do you check?

- ✅ a) The AES50 cable between the X32 and the S32
- b) Every mic's battery
- c) Each channel's GAIN

✅ **Answer.** Mics come in at the X32, so the meters still move. Everything goes out through the S32 over AES50: no link, no speakers.

### Inputs: LOCAL vs. AES50

- **ROUTING → INPUT**: CH 1–24 = **==LOCAL==**, CH 25–32 = **AES50 A** (the S32)
- Wireless ==receivers== plug into the X32's **own** rear panel → LOCAL
- CME's band: sub snake → S32 IN 25–32 → AES50
- Last event left 1–24 on AES50? Then **no mic arrives**. Check it ==first==

**Q15 (fill in the blank).** Salmon's wireless receivers plug into the X32's own rear panel, so CH 1–24 are on ==LOCAL== inputs.

✅ **Answer.** CH 1–24 are LOCAL; CH 25–32 come from the S32 over AES50. If the last event left 1–24 on AES50, no mic arrives.

### Outputs: the mains

- MAIN L/R → S32 **OUT 15/16** → the hall's ==mains rack== → the hall's speakers
- The hall's mains rack has the small **==Mackie== Mix5** on top. It's also how a teacher plugs a
  laptop or phone into the hall's speakers
- CME adds its **own**: S32 OUT 13/14 → the **Crown** at the bottom of the ==amp rack== (not the
  hall's mains rack) → two Yamaha speakers **downstage**
- Why downstage: every open mic is **==behind==** the speakers → much less ==feedback==
- CME turns the hall's speakers down at the **Mix5**: that channel's LEVEL, not MAIN MIX

**Q16 (multiple choice).** Why does CME put its own speakers downstage of the band?

- a) They're louder than the hall's speakers
- ✅ b) Every open mic is behind them, so feedback is much less likely
- c) The hall's speakers can't play a band

✅ **Answer.** Downstage of the band, the open mics are behind the speakers: much less feedback.

**Q17 (multiple choice).** Which amp drives CME's downstage speakers?

- ✅ a) The Crown at the bottom of the amp rack
- b) The Crown in the hall's mains rack
- c) The Mackie Mix5

✅ **Answer.** The Crown at the bottom of the amp rack. The hall's mains rack, with the Mix5 on top, is a different rack.

### Outputs: Vocal Jazz wedges

**MIX 1 → S32 OUT 1 → XLR → NX3000 CH A → speakON → wedge → wedge → wedge**

- One ==mix==, one amp channel, three wedges in a **==daisy chain==**
- XLR carries the mix at **==line level==**; speakON carries the amp's **power**
- Not working? Check your ==routing== and ==signal flow==

**Q18 (fill in the blank).** Vocal Jazz's three wedges share one mix and one amp channel, connected in a ==daisy chain|daisy-chain|daisychain==.

✅ **Answer.** MIX 1 → S32 OUT 1 → NX3000 CH A → speakON → wedge → wedge → wedge. Not working? Check your routing and signal flow.

### Outputs: CME wedges

- **Three mixes**: MIX 9, 10, 11 → S32 OUT 9, 10, 11
- Wedges 1 and 2 (==passive==): KM750 CH A and B → **1/4" speaker cable**
- Wedge 3 (==active==): XLR straight in, plus its own **power** and **VOLUME** knob
- Don't daisy-chain wedges on **different** mixes: every wedge in a chain plays the **==same==**
  mix, so three mixes need three amp channels
- **==Speaker== cable** (thick) is not ==instrument== cable (thin): same plug, different job
- Side note: in-ear monitors (==IEMs==) are possible later. We don't use them right now

**Q19 (multiple choice).** Why does CME give each wedge its own amp channel instead of a daisy chain?

- ✅ a) Every wedge in a chain plays the same mix, and CME has three mixes
- b) speakON cables can't be chained
- c) The active wedge needs XLR

✅ **Answer.** A chain carries one mix. Three different mixes need three amp channels.

### Gain staging: the whole chain

| Stage | Control | Its one job |
|---|---|---|
| Preamp | **==GAIN==** | How hot the signal comes **in** |
| Channel | **==Fader==** | The mix in the mains: who's louder than whom |
| Send | **Send level** | How much of this channel goes into a wedge mix |
| Bus | **MIX / MAIN ==master==** | How much of that whole mix leaves |
| Amp | **Level knob** | Set **once**, during the music test |
| Speaker | (active wedge: VOLUME) | Up, and left there |

- The fader sets the mains; the **==send==** sets each wedge. On the X32: **SENDS ON FADERS**
- Fix a level where it's **wrong**: too hot coming in → **GAIN**, not the fader
- Every stage up and ==unmuted==, or nothing comes out

**Q20 (fill in the blank).** A singer wants more of their own voice in their wedge, not in the house. You raise that channel's ==send|send level==.

✅ **Answer.** The fader sets the mains; the send sets each wedge. On the X32: SENDS ON FADERS.

**Q21 (multiple choice).** A channel is clipping as it comes in. What do you turn down?

- a) The fader
- ✅ b) GAIN
- c) The amp's level knob

✅ **Answer.** Fix a level where it's wrong: too hot coming in is a GAIN problem, not a fader problem.

### Gain staging: Vocal Jazz

- Every wireless channel at the **==same==** gain: **==30 dB==**
- The group balances **itself**, like an ensemble should
- A quiet singer is a conversation with the director, not a knob
- **==Low cut==** at 80 Hz; no gate, no ==compressor==

**Q22 (multiple choice).** Why does every Vocal Jazz wireless channel get the same gain (30 dB)?

- ✅ a) So the group balances itself, like an ensemble
- b) So the meters line up neatly
- c) So the wedges can be louder

✅ **Answer.** With identical gains the balance is the singers' own. A quiet singer is a conversation with the director, not a knob.

### Gain staging: CME

- **GAIN** each channel into the ==good== range: peaks about **−18 to −6**
- **==48V==** on the piano's two pencil condensers (CH 27, 28)
- Save your levels: a ==level sheet==, an input list, a saved ==scene==, or photos
- The point: the **gains stay the ==same==** from soundcheck through rehearsal and the show

**Q23 (multiple choice).** Where should a CME channel's peaks land once its GAIN is set?

- a) Right at 0
- b) About −40 to −30
- ✅ c) About −18 to −6

✅ **Answer.** GAIN into the good range, peaks about −18 to −6. The faders are for the mix.

**Q24 (short answer).** Name two ways to save CME's levels, and why you save them.

✅ **Answer.** A level sheet, an input list, a saved scene, or photos. So the gains stay the same from soundcheck through rehearsal and the show.

### Test in order

1. Music you know through the **==mains==**
2. House down, then music through **each ==wedge==**
3. Amp knobs set, about the same on every channel, **while the music plays**
4. Then the mics, one at a time: the meter, the mains, the wedges

- Each wedge is checked for **==signal==** *and* **==level==**: hearing it is step one. Then watch
  what the meter shows, and how loud it actually looks and feels on stage
- In the mains but not the wedges? The mic is fine: look at the **==monitor== path**

**Q25 (multiple choice).** Music is playing through a wedge and you can hear it. Is that wedge checked?

- ✅ a) Not yet: check its level too, on the meter and by how loud it feels
- b) Yes: hearing it is enough
- c) Only once a singer has tried it

✅ **Answer.** Each wedge is checked for signal and for level. Hearing it is step one.
