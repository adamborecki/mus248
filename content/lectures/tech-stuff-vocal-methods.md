### Live Sound: The Big Picture

**==Signal flow==:** the path audio takes from its source to the speakers.

Microphone → Mixer → Amplifier → Speakers

- **==Mixer==:** combines and controls multiple audio signals.
- **==Channel==:** one audio signal on the mixer (usually one mic = one channel).
- **==Gain==:** controls the input signal level. Set first, before anything else.
- **==Fader==:** controls how much of a signal goes into a mix.

### Cables (the Short Version)

- **==XLR==:** three-pin connector for microphones. This is the one singers handle.
- **¼-inch:** instrument and line cables. Looks like a speaker cable, but they are NOT
  ==interchangeable==.

**DI box, snake, stage box:** ways of getting instruments and lots of mics to the mixer. On a
bigger stage, your mic plugs into a box onstage, not straight into the mixer.

### Mic Technique

- **Distance:** a few inches from your mouth. Too far: the engineer turns you up, and with you
  comes everything else onstage.
- **==Proximity== effect:** closer = more bass. Use it on purpose for warmth; back off for loud,
  high notes.
- **Aim:** sing *into* the top of the mic, not across it.
- **Don't ==cup== the mic.** Holding the grille makes it boomy and invites feedback.
- **Don't tap or blow** into it to check it. Say "check, one, two" or sing a phrase.
- **Hold it steady.** The mic doesn't know you moved your head.

> 🧑‍🏫 Demo: same phrase at 1 inch, 6 inches, 1 foot, and cupped. Let the class hear it.

### Feedback

**==Feedback==:** the squeal when sound from the speakers goes back into the mic and gets
amplified again and again.

How singers cause it, and avoid it:

- Don't point the mic at a speaker or a monitor.
- Don't walk in front of the main speakers with the mic.
- Don't cup the grille.
- If it starts: back away from the speaker and lower the mic. Then tell the engineer.

### Monitors: Hearing Yourself

- **FOH (==Front of House==):** the main speakers, for the audience.
- **==Monitor==:** a speaker (wedge) or in-ear system aimed at the performers so they can hear.

Asking for a monitor mix:

- Be specific: "more ==me==," "less piano," "a little more of the alto mic."
- Ask between songs, not in the middle of one.
- Use hand signals the engineer can see: point at yourself, thumb up.

**Stage volume matters!** If everyone keeps turning up, the whole room gets louder and the mix
gets harder to control.

### Protect Your Voice

- Can't hear yourself? Ask for more monitor. Don't ==oversing== to compensate.
- Reverb sounds lovely, but too much hides pitch problems from *you*.
- Loud stages tire your ears and your voice. Earplugs and in-ears are tools, not cheating.

### Line Check vs. Soundcheck vs. Rehearsal

- **==Line Check==:** does everything work? Is each mic reaching the mixer?
- **==Soundcheck==:** does everything sound good? Are levels and monitors right?
- **==Rehearsal==:** can we actually perform the music together?

**At soundcheck, sing like the show.** If you check quietly and perform loudly, the levels are
wrong for the show.

### Be Prepared

- Arrive ==early==. Be ready to start at the scheduled time.
- Know your music ==before== rehearsal.
- Bring what you need: music, water, your own in-ears if you use them.
- Communicate clearly and respond to messages.
- Listen to the sound engineer. They are on your ==team==.

**Remember:** being on time and being ready to sing are not the same thing.

### Be Someone People Want to Work With

There are LOTS of talented singers in the world.

- Be ==reliable==.
- Be flexible.
- Respect other musicians and technical staff.
- Own your mistakes and learn from them.
- Be kind, even when things go wrong.

Being talented gets you ==noticed==. Being professional gets you invited ==back==.

### AI: Use Your Brain

AI can be genuinely helpful, but think carefully about when and how you use it.

**Good uses:**

- Scheduling and planning
- Drafting emails
- Organizing notes and administrative work
- Helping you understand unfamiliar concepts

**Be careful about:**

- Relying on AI instead of developing your own ==skills==
- Outsourcing your creativity without thinking
- Trusting AI answers without ==verification==

**Bottom line:** let AI handle the ==tedious== stuff. Keep developing the skills, judgment, and
creativity that make you a musician.

### Your Turn: Listen

One mic runs through the **Zoom V3** vocal processor. Sing the same phrase each time and listen
for the difference:

- **High frequencies:** brightness, air, the "s" sounds. Too much: harsh.
- **Low frequencies:** warmth, body. Too much: boomy, muddy.
- **==Compression==:** evens out loud and soft. Quiet notes come up, loud notes come down.
- **==Echo==:** distinct repeats of what you just sang.
- **==Delay==:** the effect that makes echoes; short delays thicken, long delays repeat.
- **==Reverb==:** the sound of a room or hall around your voice.

**Listen first, then name it.** What changed: brighter, darker, more even, bigger room?
