### Slide 4: The Big Picture

MUS 248 engineers support real performances across several rooms and several types of work.

The specific gear changes from gig to gig. The core responsibilities do not.

A single gig may include:

- Audio recording
- Video recording
- Multicamera switching
- Livestreaming
- Live sound (FOH)
- Portable PA
- Data management and post-production

**Q1 (multiple choice).** The specific gear changes from gig to gig. What doesn't?

- a) The venue
- ✅ b) The core responsibilities
- c) The size of the crew
- d) The list of equipment

✅ **Answer.** Gear changes from gig to gig; the core responsibilities don't. A single gig can mix
audio, video, multicam switching, livestreaming, FOH, a portable PA, and data and post.

### Slide 5: The Workflow for Every Gig

**Before the gig**

- Know the venue, call time, concert, and required services.
- Gather and check gear before leaving.
- Arrive in appropriate attire (closed toed shoes, no logos on shirts, all black if Musco)
- Arrive fed and caffeinated.

**During the gig**

- Communicate clearly.
- Ask early about anything that depends on house staff, lighting, performers, or venue access.
- Don't get lazy. Check & Double Check everything
- Protect the performance: stay quiet, stay organized, and solve problems without disrupting the event.

**After the gig**

- Stop and power down the equipment you used.
- Transfer and verify media.
- Return gear ready for the next engineer.

**Post Production**

- Complete post-production and delivery as assigned.

### Slide 14: Standard Gear Kit: Audio Workflow

**Main stereo recording:**

- Put the pencil condensers on the stereo bar.
- Use ORTF or NOS. The checklist notes that NOS is usually easier. If you're putting a backup recorder in the middle. If using a separate stand, use ORTF.
- Left microphone to F8 Input 1.
- Right microphone to F8 Input 2.
- If needed, board feed to Inputs 3 and 4.
- Use PFL to confirm signal and set gain.
- Use phantom power only where the condensers need it.
- Enable dual channel recording on inputs 1-4, so that 5/6 is a copy of 1/2 and 7/8 is a copy of ¾
- ensure gain is identical for stereo pairs, and approximately 12-20dB quieter for dual channel recording channels.

**Backup recording:**

- Mount the H4n on its lighting stand.
- Use charged batteries and a clean SD card.
- If possible, check the loudest passage. If not possible, set conservatively.
- As always: avoid clipping and recordings that are too quiet.

IF using boundary microphones, put into 3/4 or 5/6. Check Dual channel!

### Slide 15: Standard Gear Kit: F8 and Camera Starting Points

**Zoom F8:**

- Use AC power for the concert.
- Batteries are backup only.
- Enable Dual Channel Recording.
- Main microphones: channels 1 and 2.
- Dual-channel copies: channels 5 and 6.
- If using a board feed, the checklist assigns it to inputs 3 and 4, with copies on 7 and 8.
- Checklist starting points: approximately 42 dB on channels 1–2 and 30 dB on 5–6.
- These are starting points, not automatic final settings. But if you don't have time to soundcheck, these settings will be fine.

**AX100 cameras:**

- Ask the house staff for "Concert Lighting" (whatever the brightest lighting cue will be).
- Match both cameras to "Indoor" white balance (set it to "Indoor", or manually set to 3200K)
- Starting exposure: f/4.0, 3 dB gain, 1/60 shutter.
- Set exposure under actual concert lighting. Pre-show is usually much much darker.
- Keep the cameras level and frame deliberately. (avoid rotation!)
