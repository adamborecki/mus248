### Slide 4: The Big Picture

MUS 248 engineers support real performances across several rooms and several types of work.

The specific gear changes from gig to gig. The core responsibilities do not.

A single gig may include:

- Audio recording
- Video recording
- Multicamera switching
- Livestreaming
- Live sound (FOH)
- Portable PA
- Data management and post-production

### Slide 5: The Workflow for Every Gig

**Before the gig**

- Know the venue, call time, concert, and required services.
- Gather and check gear before leaving.
- Arrive in appropriate attire (closed toed shoes, no logos on shirts, all black if Musco)
- Arrive fed and caffeinated.

**During the gig**

- Communicate clearly.
- Ask early about anything that depends on house staff, lighting, performers, or venue access.
- Don't get lazy. Check & Double Check everything
- Protect the performance: stay quiet, stay organized, and solve problems without disrupting the event.

**After the gig**

- Stop and power down the equipment you used.
- Transfer and verify media.
- Return gear ready for the next engineer.

**Post Production**

- Complete post-production and delivery as assigned.

### Slide 6: The 7 Gig Families (categories)

1. Salmon standard A/V and livestream
2. Salmon FOH
3. Musco
4. Standard Gear Kit recordings
5. Portable live sound
6. Studio and multitrack recording
7. Specialty productions

A venue does not automatically determine the service. For example, Salmon may involve recording, livestreaming, FOH, or a combination.

### Slide 7: Salmon Standard A/V and Livestream

Typical work:

- Student recitals
- Faculty and guest recitals
- Chamber and ensemble events
- Music at Noon
- Other Salmon events

Core expectation:

- Standard A/V recording is the normal Salmon workflow.
- Livestreaming is built into the standard Salmon workflow, we always practice livestreaming even if they didn't request it. But in that case, don't make it live.

The [Salmon Gig Checklist](../../salmon-gig-checklist/) is the operational reference. Please use the gig checklist, even if you're a veteran, as a way to double check.

### Slide 8: 2. Salmon FOH (Front of House)

FOH work includes:

- Inputs and gain structure
- PA and monitor routing
- Line check and soundcheck
- Playback and microphones as needed
- Listening for problems and responding calmly

Primary examples:

- Mariachi
- Commercial Music Ensemble
- Vocal Jazz
- Occasionally Music at Noon

The goal is a safe, reliable working system. Advanced mixing skill can develop over time, but every student needs the basic operating skills.

### Slide 10: 3. Musco: Standard and Advanced Recording

**Standard Musco recording:**

- Backup recording (with H4N and AX100 from standard gear kit)

Bring 3x SSD's:

- 1 SSD captures the "Program" (multicam)
- 1 SSD captures an ISO of a "Static Wide"
- 1 SSD captures an ISO of a "Conductor Camera"
- Data transfer after the gig

"ISO" is sorta the video equivalent of "multitrack stems". An "ISO" is a single camera video feed that is recorded. In contrast to the "Program" (multicam), which is an edit between angles, the ISO should be the same camera output for the entire recording. (sometimes we have a second conductor camera on stage as a tripod in addition to the house conductor camera)

**Advanced Musco recording:**

- Big Band and Jazz Combo
- Typically two concerts per year
- Multicamera switching (as normal)
- Audio folks go down to the basement to use the CL5 for real time multitrack live mixing.

OH 207 has a CL3, the smaller version of the CL5, so students who work the big band and jazz combo gig should prepare by practicing on the CL3 in OH 207.

### Slide 12: Standard Gear Kit: What It Is

The Standard Gear Kit is our portable concert-recording rig.

Its core setup:

- Stereo pair of pencil condenser microphones into a Zoom F8
- Zoom H4n Pro or similar recorder for backup audio
- Two Sony AX100 cameras

Use it for gigs where we bring recording gear with us.

[Standard Gear Kit](../../standard-gear-kit-checklist/): see that document for gig checklist and also link to a google doc with pictures.

### Slide 13: Standard Gear Kit: Equipment List

**Audio:**

- Telescoping mic stand
- Stereo bar and two mic clips with correct screw sizes, probably 3/8"
- 2 cardioid pencil condenser microphones (KM 184)
- 2 or 4 XLR cables for the microphones
- Two additional XLR cables if using a board feed, ideally with XLR to TRS adapters so it goes into the F8 as ¼" cables.
- Zoom F8 with AC power, backup batteries, and a clean 32 GB SD card.
- Zoom H4n Pro or similar with charged batteries and a clean 8 GB SD card
- Lighting stand with a ¼-inch screw for the H4n
- Velcro ties

**Video:**

- Two Sony AX100 cameras
- Correct tripod plate for each AX100
- Two tripods
- Two clean 128 GB SD cards
- Charged camera batteries or AC power

### Slide 14: Standard Gear Kit: Audio Workflow

**Main stereo recording:**

- Put the pencil condensers on the stereo bar.
- Use ORTF or NOS. The checklist notes that NOS is usually easier. If you're putting a backup recorder in the middle. If using a separate stand, use ORTF.
- Left microphone to F8 Input 1.
- Right microphone to F8 Input 2.
- If needed, board feed to Inputs 3 and 4.
- Use PFL to confirm signal and set gain.
- Use phantom power only where the condensers need it.
- Enable dual channel recording on inputs 1-4, so that 5/6 is a copy of 1/2 and 7/8 is a copy of ¾
- ensure gain is identical for stereo pairs, and approximately 12-20dB quieter for dual channel recording channels.

**Backup recording:**

- Mount the H4n on its lighting stand.
- Use charged batteries and a clean SD card.
- If possible, check the loudest passage. If not possible, set conservatively.
- As always: avoid clipping and recordings that are too quiet.

IF using boundary microphones, put into 3/4 or 5/6. Check Dual channel!

### Slide 15: Standard Gear Kit: F8 and Camera Starting Points

**Zoom F8:**

- Use AC power for the concert.
- Batteries are backup only.
- Enable Dual Channel Recording.
- Main microphones: channels 1 and 2.
- Dual-channel copies: channels 5 and 6.
- If using a board feed, the checklist assigns it to inputs 3 and 4, with copies on 7 and 8.
- Checklist starting points: approximately 42 dB on channels 1–2 and 30 dB on 5–6.
- These are starting points, not automatic final settings. But if you don't have time to soundcheck, these settings will be fine.

**AX100 cameras:**

- Ask the house staff for "Concert Lighting" (whatever the brightest lighting cue will be).
- Match both cameras to "Indoor" white balance (set it to "Indoor", or manually set to 3200K)
- Starting exposure: f/4.0, 3 dB gain, 1/60 shutter.
- Set exposure under actual concert lighting. Pre-show is usually much much darker.
- Keep the cameras level and frame deliberately. (avoid rotation!)

### Slide 19: Data Management and Post-Production

Every engineer needs to be able to:

- Get a copy of the program
- Keep a clear folder structure and useful file names that follow our conventions.
- Transfer media to the assigned destinations. (2 destinations)
- Put a program on the hard drives in addition to the files
- Confirm files copied correctly before anything is erased.
- Properly check then fully delete (empty the trash!)
- Help out – Finish data transfer for unfinished files (e.g. if someone else started but had to leave before cleaning)

Post production:

- Fast turnaround time
- Clear communication when assigned post
