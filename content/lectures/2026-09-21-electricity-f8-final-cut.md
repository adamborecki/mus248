### Slide 28: Lecture 04: Part 1 — Electricity

### Slide 29: Electricity: AC vs DC

**AC = Alternating Current**

- Used for wall power.
- In the United States, typical wall power is about 120V AC at 60 Hz.
- The familiar three-prong wall plug is often casually called an "Edison" plug or "AC" in audio/production environments.

**DC = Direct Current**

- Batteries provide DC.
- Many pieces of audio equipment operate on DC.
- A power adapter often converts AC from the wall into the DC that a device needs.

### Slide 30: Electricity: AC POWER CABLES

- **Hot:** Carries electrical power.
- **Neutral:** Provides the normal return path.
- **Ground:** Safety connection. Helps protect people if something goes wrong.

**Important:** Never remove or defeat the ground pin to solve an audio problem.

Cables: 2 pin IEC · 3 pin IEC

### Slide 31: Electricity: Power Adapters

Just because a power adapter fits physically does NOT mean it is safe to use. Before connecting an adapter, check:

**AC or DC:** The adapter must provide the type of power the device requires.

**Voltage:** Voltage must match. Device requires 12V:

- 12V adapter = YES
- 9V adapter = NO
- 15V adapter = NO

**Polarity:** For DC barrel connectors, check whether the device is center positive or center negative. Polarity must match.

**Amperage / Current:** The adapter must provide AT LEAST the amount of current the device requires. Device requires 12V DC, 1A. Adapter:

- 12V, 0.5A = NO
- 12V, 1A = YES
- 12V, 2A = YES

A 2A adapter does not "force" 2 amps into the device. The device draws the current it needs. 1000 mA = 1A

**QUICK RULE.** Before plugging in a power adapter, check:

- AC or DC?
- Voltage matches?
- Polarity matches?
- Enough amperage?
- Connector actually fits?

### Slide 34: Lecture 04: Part 2 — Zoom F8

### Slide 35: Zoom F8

**What is it?**

- Portable multitrack recorder
- 8 XLR inputs
- Records to SD card

**Power**

- 8 AA batteries
- Or 12V DC power

**For each microphone:**

1. Plug into the correct input
2. Turn on the track (This RECORD ENABLES it)
3. Press PFL
4. Check/set gain
5. Turn on +48V phantom power if needed
6. Watch the meter (check for peaks)
7. Listen on headphones
8. Record and verify

**Important:** PFL lets you focus on one channel and see the exact gain setting.

### Slide 36: Zoom F8: Dual Channel Recording

- Input 1 can also record to Track 5
- Input 2 → Track 6
- Input 3 → Track 7
- Input 4 → Track 8

**Why?**

- The second track can be recorded at a lower level as a safety track.
- If the main track clips, the quieter copy may still be usable.

**Remember:** Track 5 is not always Input 5.

**RECORD ≠ DONE.** Always verify:

- Are meters moving?
- Does it sound good in headphones?
- Did the recording actually save?

### Slide 37: Lecture 04: Part 3 — Intro to Final Cut

### Slide 38: Intro to FCP

**What is Final Cut Pro?**

- Video editing software
- We use it to combine picture and our better recorded audio
- Think of it as the "video side" of post production

**The basic workflow:**

1. Create/open a Library AND RENAME CLIPS
2. Create a Project AND SET TO "LEAVE FILES IN PLACE"
3. Import your video and audio
4. Put clips on the timeline
5. Sync the good audio to the camera video
6. Trim the beginning and end
7. Add fades as needed
8. Check the result
9. Export

**Three important areas:**

- Browser = where your imported media lives
- Viewer = where you watch
- Timeline = where you edit

**Basic timeline skills:**

- Zoom in/out so you can make precise edits
- Move around the timeline
- Select clips
- Cut at the playhead
- Trim clips
- Adjust audio level
- Add audio/video fades

### Slide 39: Using Final Cut Pro at Chapman

For our workflow:

- Import using File → Import
- Choose "Leave files in place"
- Set the project to 1080p for individual movements or clips
- Disable Background Render
- Keep your original files organized outside Final Cut
- Export video as H.264, NOT ProRes

**Most important concept:** Final Cut is usually NOT changing your original recording files. You are creating instructions for: "Use this part of this file, starting here, ending here, at this level." Then when you export, Final Cut creates the finished file.

**For Phase 1:** VIDEO + GOOD AUDIO → SYNC → TRIM → FADE → QC "Quality Control" → EXPORT H.264 VIDEO + WAV AUDIO
