## 🎤 Lecture: Live Sound Part 1 — Signals, Amplification, and Mixer Basics

### Four Weeks of Live Sound

A working sequence. Each week keeps the same signal-flow vocabulary and adds a new layer.

1. **Small systems:** Inputs, outputs, signal levels, preamps, power amps, active and passive speakers.
2. **Destinations:** Aux sends, monitor mixes, PFL/Solo, pre-fader and post-fader thinking.
3. **System workflow:** Stage patching, channel organization, gain staging, DI boxes, line check.
4. **Troubleshooting and feeds:** Fault tracing, stereo board feed, multitrack feed, reset and handoff.

### The Small-System Mental Model

Students leave able to describe the system before they touch a particular brand of mixer.

A mixer receives sources, makes decisions about level and destination, then sends useful signals onward.

- **Inputs:** What enters the mixer, and at what level?
- **Channel path:** Where does mic gain become usable line-level signal?
- **Outputs:** What leaves the mixer, and what does the receiving device expect?
- **Amplification:** Where does speaker-level power actually get created?

### An Analog Mixer: One Layout, Many Functions

Use a single product photo to practice visual orientation, not memorization of this exact model.

- **MIC INPUTS:** XLR inputs at the top left. These feed microphone preamps.
- **LINE INPUTS:** 1/4-inch and stereo line inputs accept other source types.
- **CHANNEL CONTROLS:** Gain occurs early; EQ, aux, pan, mute, and fader occur along the path.
- **OUTPUTS:** Main, aux, control-room, phones, and record connections do different jobs.

### Signal Categories

Treat these as practical categories, not one fixed voltage for every piece of equipment.

| Category | Description |
|---|---|
| MIC | Very small microphone signal |
| INSTRUMENT | Pickup / high-impedance source |
| LINE | The usual mixer-to-powered-device signal |
| SPEAKER | Power-amplified signal for a passive speaker |

Connector shape does not settle the question. An XLR or 1/4-inch connection can carry different signals in different situations.

### Preamp and Power Amp

These are different jobs, even when one product contains both.

Microphone → Preamp → Mixer / line output → Power amp → Passive speaker

- **Microphone:** Mic level.
- **Preamp:** Brings a small source up.
- **Mixer / line output:** Line level.
- **Power amp:** Provides speaker-driving power.
- **Passive speaker:** Speaker level.

Question for every speaker system: where does the system create the power needed to move the loudspeaker?

*Diagram note: This is a conceptual sequence, not a wiring prescription for all systems.*

### Active and Passive Speaker Systems

The physical product tells you what kind of signal it expects and whether it needs AC power.

- **Powered Studio Monitors:** AC power + line input. The power amp lives in the cabinet.
- **M-Audio DX4:** The primary cabinet contains the inputs/amplification and drives its matching passive partner.
- **Passive Wedge:** Requires a separate power amp between the mixer output and speaker.

**Do not connect a mixer line output directly to a passive loudspeaker.**

*🖼️ Photos in original slide. Equipment note: Instructor-provided campus photos. Campus notes identify the studio-monitor collection as powered. The M-Audio rear label identifies Studiophile DX4. Verify the exact matched cabinet/supply pairing before use.*

### Common Connector Types

Connector type is a physical clue. Confirm the signal and the destination before you patch.

| Connector | Common use |
|---|---|
| XLR | Common for microphone connections and balanced line connections |
| 1/4-inch TRS | Balanced mono or unbalanced stereo, depending on the circuit |
| 1/4-inch TS | Often instrument or unbalanced mono connections |
| 3.5 mm TRS | Common laptop / phone stereo playback connection |
| RCA | Common consumer unbalanced stereo connection |
| SpeakON | Locking speaker-level connector between amp and passive speaker |

**Important:** connector "terminator" alone does not define signal type.

*🖼️ Image in original slide. Connector reference image source: EET China. Used only as a visual reference for connector form. Slide text supplies the English instructional labels and caveats.*

### Inputs and Outputs

Different source types enter a mixer. The mixer sends the right signal to an appropriate output system.

- **Microphone:** E.g. Dynamic or Condenser (requires 48V). XLR / mic level.
- **Laptop playback:** 3.5 mm to appropriate line input.
- **Mixer:** Gain, channel level, output selection.
- **Powered monitor:** Line input + AC power.
- **Power amp:** Line in, then speaker-level out.
- **Passive speaker:** Signal from power amp.

The mixer output stays at line level. The powered monitor or power amp handles the next job.

### Week 1 Outcome

Students can walk up to an unfamiliar compact mixer and reason through the first safe steps.

1. Identify a mic input, a line/stereo input, a main output, and a monitoring output.
2. Explain why a microphone needs a preamp and a passive speaker needs a power amp.
3. Recognize a powered output system versus a passive loudspeaker system.
4. Get a microphone and a playback source through a mixer to an appropriate useful output.
5. Preserve equipment provenance and avoid mismatched power supplies or unknown patching.
