## 🎤 Lecture: Live Sound Part 2 — Destinations: Main Mixes, Aux Sends, and Monitor Mixes

### Live Sound Part 2: Destinations

**Last week:** Inputs, outputs, levels, preamps, power amps, and active versus passive speakers.

**This week:** One source can go to more than one destination.

### The Question for Today

A microphone can be audible in the room and still be missing from a performer's monitor.

Why?

Because the main mix and monitor mix are different destinations.

### A Mixer Does More Than Make One Mix

A channel can feed:

- **Main L/R:** The audience mix.
- **Aux / Monitor mix:** A performer's speaker.
- **Headphones / control room:** The engineer's listening path.
- **Recording or streaming feed:** A separate destination.
- **PFL / Solo:** A troubleshooting/listening path.

A **bus** is an internal mix path. An **output connector** is where that path physically leaves the mixer.

### A Mixer Does More Than Make One Mix…?

In big productions, you have multiple mixing engineers:

- **FOH:** Engineer specifically dialing in mix for audience. At Front of House.
- **Monitor Mixer:** Engineer specifically focused on mixes for musicians on stage. At side stage.

If there is a separate monitor mixer, they probably have their own console (with stems shared with FOH console, probably via Dante or similar) or at the very least an iPad that remote controls the main console.

### Main L/R

Main L/R usually feeds the audience system.

For a small system: Mic or playback source → channel → Main L/R → powered speaker

For a passive system: Mic or playback source → channel → Main L/R → power amp → passive speaker

The main mix answers: **What should the audience hear?**

### Aux Sends

An Aux send takes some of a channel and sends it to another mix.

Lead vocal channel → Aux 1 send ("copy") → Aux 1 bus → Aux output → monitor

Each channel can have a different amount sent to the Aux.

This is why a drummer can ask for more vocal without making the vocal louder for the audience.

### Monitor Mixes

A monitor mix answers: **What does this performer need to hear?**

**A Singer Might Want**

- Lots of their own voice
- Some keyboard
- A little guitar
- Less drums

**A Drummer Might Want**

- More drums and bass
- Some lead vocal
- Less guitar

Neither mix has to match the audience mix.

### Pro Advice

Making a monitor mix?

- Test it for (1) correct signal flow and (2) reasonable volume level. Play some audio, e.g. wi channel theme, BEFORE the band arrives.
- Start with NOTHING in the monitors, add things.
- COMMUNICATE with the artists if anything changes in the monitor. Probably don't change it unless they ask.
- If you make a "mistake" or something (e.g. alter preamp gain) that will effect monitor, be smart about what to do (e.g. compensate for exact amount so result is the same, do another test with the artists).

### "More of Me" Versus "Everything Is Too Quiet"

These are different problems.

- **"More of My Voice":** Raise that channel's Aux send.
- **"My Entire Monitor Is Too Quiet":** Raise the Aux master/output level.

The first changes one source inside the monitor mix. The second changes the whole monitor mix.

**PRO TIP:** Artists often don't know this difference. It's up to YOU to figure out what the correct course of action is.

### Pre-Fader and Post-Fader Sends

A send can tap the channel before or after the channel fader.

- **Pre-Fader:** The monitor send stays the same when FOH changes the channel fader. Useful for performer monitors.
- **Post-Fader:** The send follows the channel fader. Useful for effects and some recording/streaming situations.

Do not assume every Aux is configured the same way. Check the mixer.

### Example: Vocal Too Loud in the House

The singer is happy with their monitor. The audience says the vocal is too loud.

If the singer's monitor send is pre-fader:

- Lower the vocal in Main L/R.
- The singer's monitor level stays about the same.

This is one reason monitor mixes often use pre-fader sends.

### PFL, Solo, and Cue

Different mixers use different labels:

- **PFL:** Pre-fader listen — VERY COMMON
- **AFL:** After-fader listen
- **Solo**
- **Cue**

The basic purpose stays the same: listen to or meter a signal without necessarily sending it to the audience.

Use this for setup and troubleshooting before turning up the room.

### Main Mix, Aux Mix, and PFL Compared

| Destination | Listener | Typical question |
|---|---|---|
| Main L/R | Audience | What should the room hear? |
| Aux / Monitor | Performer | What does this person need? |
| PFL / Solo | Engineer | Is this channel receiving usable signal? |
| Recording feed "board feed" | Recorder / stream | What should the recording receive? |

### Physical Output Paths Still Matter

An Aux send is only an internal mix until it reaches a physical output.

Powered monitor: Aux 1 bus → Aux 1 output → powered monitor

Passive monitor: Aux 1 bus → Aux 1 output → power amp → passive monitor

The Aux output from the mixer is still line level.

### Week 2 Practice Goal

Starting with a working Main mix:

1. Identify an Aux send and Aux output.
2. Connect the Aux output to a safe second speaker system.
3. Send one source to that second speaker.
4. Change one source in the Aux mix without changing Main L/R.
5. Raise the Aux master without changing the internal balance.
