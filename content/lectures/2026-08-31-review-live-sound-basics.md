### Slide 71: Review: Last Week's Lecture

**Professionalism = reliability.** Be on time, communicate early, stay engaged, take care of equipment, help your group, finish the job, practice basic hygiene, and be someone people can trust.

**Five Core Skill Areas:** Audio Capture, Video Capture, Live Sound, Data Management & Archiving, Post Production.

**Audio basics:** Know your signal flow, set the correct I/O, arm the right track, use 48V when appropriate, set gain carefully, avoid clipping, check mono vs. stereo, listen back, record a test, and know where files are being saved.

**Video basics:** Set exposure manually, understand aperture/iris + shutter speed + ISO/gain, set frame rate intentionally, set white balance manually, check focus and framing, and record a test before the real event.

**Big picture:** Before recording, you should know where the signal/image is coming from, where it is going, whether the settings are appropriate, and whether you are actually capturing what you think you are.

**MUS 248 goal:** Build enough technical skill and reliability that you can eventually handle recording work without needing constant supervision.

## 🎤 Lecture: Live Sound

### Slide 72: Live Sound

- **Know the signal flow.** Source → microphone/DI/playback device → mixer → amplifier (if needed) → speakers.
- **Set gain first.** Use the input gain/preamp to get a healthy signal before relying on the channel fader or master fader.
- **Mains = audience mix.** Main speakers should cover the audience and are usually placed in front of the microphones to reduce feedback.
- **Monitors = performer mix.** Stage monitors let performers hear themselves; a custom monitor mix may be different from the main mix.
- **Watch for feedback.** Feedback happens when sound from a speaker gets back into a microphone and loops through the system again. Speaker placement, mic direction, level, and EQ all matter.
- **Know active vs. passive speakers.** Active/powered speakers have an amplifier built in; passive speakers require an external power amplifier.
- **Use phantom power when appropriate.** Condenser microphones and some active DI boxes may require 48V.
- **Know your outputs.** Before turning things up, make sure you know what is feeding the mains, monitors, recordings, and any other outputs.
- **Power up/down safely.** Speakers ON last; speakers OFF first. This helps prevent loud pops and other unpleasant surprises.
- **Make changes deliberately.** Before moving a fader, changing routing, or turning on 48V, know what that control affects.
- **Test before the real event.** Check every input, main output, monitor, and important playback source while you still have time to troubleshoot.

**Basic rule:** Before the show starts, you should know where every important signal comes from, where it goes, who needs to hear it, and which controls change it.
