### Live Sound: The Big Picture

**Signal flow:** the path audio takes from its ==source== to the speakers.

Microphone → Stage Box → Mixer → Speakers

- **Mixer:** combines and controls multiple audio signals. Most of the audio cables eventually
  connect here, directly or indirectly.
- **Channel:** an individual audio signal controlled by the mixer.
- **Gain:** controls the ==input== signal level.
- **Fader:** controls how much of a signal goes into a ==mix==.

> 🧑‍🏫 Photo: a live sound mixer.

### Cables and Connectors

- **XLR:** common three-==pin== connector used for microphones and balanced audio.
- **¼-inch TS:** Tip-Sleeve. Common for instrument cables and some speaker cables.
- **¼-inch TRS:** Tip-==Ring==-Sleeve. Often used for balanced mono or stereo audio.

**Important:** instrument cables and speaker cables may look identical, but they are NOT
==interchangeable==. Speaker cables are designed to handle much higher ==power==.

> 🧑‍🏫 Photos: XLR, ¼-inch TS, and ¼-inch TRS connectors, side by side if you can.

### DI Boxes, Snakes, and Stage Boxes

- **DI Box (Direct Injection):** connects instruments like bass, guitar, and keyboards to a
  ==microphone== input on the mixer.
- **Snake:** a cable system carrying ==multiple== audio signals between the stage and the mixing
  position.
- **Stage Box:** the connection point onstage where microphones and other equipment plug in.

Some stage boxes are analog; others convert audio to ==digital== signals.

> 🧑‍🏫 Photos: a DI box and a stage box.

### Speakers and Amplifiers

- **Active (Powered) Speaker:** has an amplifier ==built in==.
- **Passive Speaker:** requires an external power ==amplifier==.
- **FOH (Front of House):** the main sound system for the ==audience==.
- **Monitor:** a speaker or in-ear system that helps musicians hear themselves and each other.

**Stage volume matters!** If everyone keeps turning up, the entire band gets louder, and the mix
becomes harder to control.

> 🧑‍🏫 Photos: an active speaker and a passive speaker (the back panels show the difference).

### Line Check vs. Soundcheck vs. Rehearsal

- **Line Check:** does everything ==work==? Are signals reaching the mixer?
- **Soundcheck:** does everything ==sound== good? Are levels and monitor mixes appropriate?
- **Rehearsal:** can the musicians actually ==perform== the music together?

These are three different things!

**Bonus:** ==feedback== is the familiar squealing sound caused when amplified sound re-enters a
microphone and gets amplified repeatedly.

### Be Prepared

- Arrive ==early==. Be ready to start at the scheduled time.
- Know your music ==before== rehearsal.
- Bring your instruments, cables, accessories, and anything else you need.
- Communicate clearly and respond to messages.
- Listen to the sound engineer. They are on your ==team==.

**Remember:** being on time and being ready to play are not the same thing.

### Be Someone People Want to Work With

There are LOTS of talented musicians in the world.

- Be ==reliable==.
- Be flexible.
- Respect other musicians and technical staff.
- Own your mistakes and learn from them.
- Be kind, even when things go wrong.

Being talented gets you ==noticed==. Being professional gets you invited ==back==.

Don't be the talented jerk nobody wants to work with.

### AI: Use Your Brain

AI can be genuinely helpful, but think carefully about when and how you use it.

**Good uses:**

- Scheduling and planning
- Drafting emails
- Organizing notes and administrative work
- Helping you understand unfamiliar concepts

**Be careful about:**

- Relying on AI instead of developing your own ==skills==
- Outsourcing your creativity without thinking
- Trusting AI answers without ==verification==

**Bottom line:** let AI handle the ==tedious== stuff. Keep developing the skills, judgment, and
creativity that make you a musician.
