### Purpose

Much of this course's documentation was drafted with AI help and has never been fully tested in
the room. In this assignment you test **one small section** of it against the real equipment
(or check it on paper, if you can't get to the gear), fix what's wrong or unclear, and hand in a
better version, with an honest record of what you tested and what's still unknown.

This is **verification and technical writing**, not proofreading. A revision only counts as an
improvement if your testing supports it.

### Directions

Full instructions, the starter menu, and all downloads are on the course site:
**[Documentation Builder](https://adamborecki.github.io/mus248/documentation-builder/)**

1. **Choose one section** from the starter menu, or one I assign. Note where it can be done:
   🏠 desk checks work anywhere; hands-on items need their room and gear.
2. **Download "starter + source."** It bundles the AI starter prompt with the page you're
   testing, and records the page's URL and revised date.
3. **Assign roles** (operator, documenter, peer tester) and plan when to swap.
4. **Write down what success should look or sound like** before you start.
5. **Follow the section exactly as written.** Record every place it's missing, confusing,
   contradictory, outdated, or doesn't match the gear.
6. **Draft a focused revision** with AI, by hand, or both. Keep working steps, add numbered steps
   where they're missing, and mark every change *Proposed*.
7. **Re-test the steps you changed.**
8. **Peer test.** Have someone who didn't write it follow your revision, and note where they
   pause. If no peer is available, write "Peer test: pending." Never make one up.
9. **Submit** the files listed under Deliverables.
10. **Reset** the equipment and room.

**Stuck?** Write down the exact obstacle, check a page that's already linked, ask a peer, then
ask me. If you can't get to the gear or the room, switch to a desk-check item or document the
blocked test. Never invent results, and never change a setting you've been told not to touch
just to finish.

### Deliverables

1. **Revised Markdown.** The whole document with your changes. For a source that isn't Markdown
   (a diagram's data file, an app), submit a Markdown change proposal naming the exact file and
   entry.
2. **Companion report** (`SUBMISSION_REPORT.md` template): source and revision, changes and
   reasons, tests and results, unresolved questions, peer test, your personal contribution and
   reflection, and your **AI-use disclosure** ("No AI used" if that's the case).
3. **Feedback on the assignment itself:** anything in the instructions that tripped you up.

**Didn't finish?** Submit your draft with the blocker and the next useful step. Thoughtful
incomplete work can meet expectations when your testing and the unresolved issues are documented
honestly.

### Tools

- Any AI tool you have access to is fine. The default is **PantherAI** (PantherAI.Chapman.edu),
  using the highest-numbered **Claude Sonnet** model it lists. Paste or upload the starter *and*
  your source; a chatbot can't open the website for you.
- **No AI is equally fine.** Manual editing and local tools are fine too. There's no penalty for
  not having a paid tool, or for choosing to work without AI.
- A GitHub account is **optional**. If you have one, you may also open an issue or pull request
  on the course repository using the same report fields. That's public, so use a group label and
  keep personal reflections in Canvas.

### Peer feedback

Peer testing shows you where your steps are unclear, and that should shape your revision.
Peers **don't** approve procedures or assign each other grades. Your revision is a proposal, and
I decide what becomes the approved operational document.
